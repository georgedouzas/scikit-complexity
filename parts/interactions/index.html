    Interactions

    An interaction changes the states of the elements bound to its roles. A role is a named slot, such as target, and it lists the domains of the states it accepts. Each interaction also lists the kinds of the laws it can return, and its arguments choose among them. The law kinds fix the time the interaction runs in, so the parts of one system must agree on it. get_tags() of an interaction returns the law kinds for the arguments you passed.     Comparison

    Class What it adds Choose it when Uses
    BaseInteraction Unlike BasePart, it changes the states of other parts and holds none of its own. A new kind of interaction runs its laws in a way of its own, as NeighborInteraction runs them once per edge. Choose Interaction for laws that run once for the elements the roles bind. It appears in every field through its subclasses, such as infection in an epidemic, a chemical reaction, a payment between accounts, and the coupling of oscillators.
    ConnectionInteraction Unlike Interaction, which returns rates or maps, it returns algebraic equations, which hold at every time. Elements with ports connect into a network of components, as in Modelica. Kirchhoff's laws join the pins of an electric circuit, pipes meet at a junction of a hydraulic network, and shafts join in a mechanical drive train.
    ConservationInteraction Unlike RateInteraction, which gives the rate of a state directly, it writes the rate as the divergence of a flux, so the scheme keeps the total and captures shocks. An amount moves through space without being created or destroyed, apart from a source. Cars on a road follow the traffic flow model of Lighthill and Whitham, a gas follows the Euler equations, and water in a channel follows the shallow water equations.
    CouplingInteraction Unlike PairInteraction, which runs its laws for every pair within a cutoff, it reduces over the members each member is coupled to, by edges, a group, or hyperedges, and it can either add to a rate or set the next value of a state. Every member responds to an aggregate of its neighbours. Phase oscillators, such as fireflies that flash in step or the generators of a power grid, sum the sine of their phase differences. Heat flow between the cells of a lattice and consensus in a social network sum differences of a state. Flocks of birds and schools of fish average the headings of their neighbours within a radius.
    CrossingInteraction Unlike JumpInteraction, which fires at random times, it fires at the exact time a condition on the states becomes true. A continuous run has discrete events, such as an impact, a threshold, or a stop. A bouncing ball reverses its velocity at the floor, a neuron fires and resets when its potential reaches a threshold, and a thermostat switches the heater at a set temperature.
    EulerLagrangeInteraction Unlike HamiltonianInteraction, which needs canonical momenta, it takes the energy in coordinates and velocities. The energy is easier to write than the forces, as for linked bodies. Pendulums and linkages in mechanics, electric circuits written by their energy, and the joints of a robot arm follow the equations of a Lagrangian.
    GradientInteraction Unlike RateInteraction, which takes the rate itself, it takes a potential and derives the rates from it. The dynamics descend an energy or a cost, so that one scalar function gives every rate. Particles move in a potential well, phases separate in an alloy, a neural network descends its loss, and cells sort by their adhesion energy.
    GroupingInteraction Unlike NeighborInteraction, whose edges are fixed by the space, it draws new groups at every step. Members meet in random pairs or small groups, such as trades or games, rather than along fixed links. Agents trade in random pairs, players of a public goods game form groups each round, and animals meet in random encounters that spread information.
    HamiltonianInteraction Unlike EulerLagrangeInteraction, which takes coordinates and velocities, it takes coordinates and momenta and gives first order rates, which symplectic solvers keep on the surface of constant energy. The energy is known in canonical form and long runs must conserve it. Planets orbit a star, atoms vibrate in a molecule, and charged particles move in a magnetic trap by Hamilton's equations.
    HyperedgeInteraction Unlike NeighborInteraction, which sees the two ends of an edge, it sees every member of a group link at once. A change depends on a whole group, such as a majority of three, and not on pairs alone. Group conversations shift opinions, a disease spreads within households and classrooms, and three species compete at once in higher-order ecological models.
    Interaction Unlike BaseInteraction, it fixes how the laws run: once per bound element or member, with no edges or pairs. A law reads the elements it binds directly, such as predation between two populations, and choose NeighborInteraction or PairInteraction when it runs along edges or between close members. Predation between two populations, a reaction between two chemical species, and a payment from a firm to its workers are each a subclass of it.
    JumpInteraction Unlike RateInteraction, which changes a state continuously, it changes states by whole amounts at random times. The units are few enough that their randomness matters, as in the stochastic simulation algorithm of Gillespie. Births and deaths change a population one at a time, customers join and leave a queue, molecules react in a small cell volume, and a machine fails and is repaired.
    LocalRuleInteraction Unlike MapInteraction, which writes the next value as an expression of one member, it reads an ordered neighbourhood and may take a table. The next value of a cell depends on the exact pattern around it. Conway's Game of Life, the sandpile of Bak, Tang, and Wiesenfeld, forest fire models, and models of traffic on a ring road are cellular automata.
    MapInteraction Unlike RateInteraction, which gives a derivative in continuous time, it gives the next value in discrete time. The model steps, such as a yearly census or a round of a game. The logistic map follows a population from year to year, an account compounds interest once a month, and agents update their wealth after each round of an exchange.
    NeighborInteraction Unlike Interaction, which runs once per bound member, it runs once per edge, so each law sees both ends of a link. A change flows along the links of a lattice or network, such as diffusion or a flow on a line. Heat flows between neighbouring cells of a lattice, a disease spreads along the contacts of a network, and power flows along the lines of a grid.
    PairInteraction Unlike NeighborInteraction, whose links are fixed edges, it finds its pairs by distance at every evaluation. Members move in continuous space and act on those close by. Atoms of a gas repel under the Lennard-Jones potential, stars attract by gravity within a cutoff, and pedestrians keep their distance in a crowd.
    RateInteraction Unlike MapInteraction, which steps a state in discrete time, it gives the derivative of a state in continuous time, with an optional noise. A state flows, as in an ordinary or a stochastic differential equation. Infected people recover at a rate, a balance grows by interest, a capacitor charges through a resistor, and a stock price follows a geometric Brownian motion.
    SamplingInteraction Unlike MapInteraction, which sets the next value by a rule, it proposes values and accepts them by a probability, so the states follow a stationary distribution. The model is a Boltzmann distribution of an energy, as in a Markov chain Monte Carlo method. Spins of a magnet in the Ising model, cells of a tissue in the cellular Potts model, and residents who move in a model of segregation are sampled this way.
    TopologyInteraction Unlike NeighborInteraction, which runs on fixed edges, it changes the edges and nodes themselves during a run. The network grows, shrinks, or rewires as the system runs, so structure and states change together. Preferential attachment grows the web and citation networks, people rewire their contacts to avoid infection, genes duplicate in the evolution of protein networks, and friends of friends close triangles in social networks.
    TrainingInteraction Unlike GradientInteraction, which moves states down a potential in the time of the system, it fits parameters of another system to data, one epoch per step. A part of the model learns from data during the run. A recurrent neural network learns to predict a time series, an echo state network fits its readout, and a learning agent improves its policy between rounds.
    TransferInteraction Unlike RateInteraction, which changes one state, it takes from one state what it adds to another, so the amount is conserved. A quantity moves between holders, such as money, people, or mass. People move from susceptible to infected in an epidemic, money flows between accounts in an economy, water flows between reservoirs, and predators gain the biomass of the prey they eat.
    TransitionInteraction Unlike JumpInteraction, which adds amounts to numeric states, it moves a categorical state between named values. Each member is in one of a few states, such as healthy, sick, and immune. A person moves from susceptible to infected in an epidemic on a network, a voter switches opinion, a cell of a forest catches fire, and a customer switches between brands.

    TransitionInteraction

    An interaction that moves a discrete state from one value to another.

    • States: declared by a subclass
    • Parameters: none declared
    • Law kinds: jump, update
    • Times: continuous, discrete
    • Solvers: CoSimulationSolver, EulerMaruyamaSolver, GillespieSolver, IterationSolver, RK4Solver, RenewalSolver
    • Backends: NumbaBackend, NumpyBackend
    • Arguments:
      • label: The name of the interaction, unique among the parts of its system, such as 'infection'.
      • target: The path of the element or group whose state moves.

      • probability: The function of the probability of the move in a step, or None.

      • emission: With a matrix, the function of the emission matrix, one row per value of the state and one column per value of the observed state, or None.

    A compatible interaction

    A RateInteraction returns a rate law of continuous time, and its target accepts the real amount of a declared element.

    from skcomplex.core.components.elements import DeclaredElement
from skcomplex.core.components.interactions import RateInteraction
from skcomplex.core.components.systems import CompositeSystem
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import simulate
from skcomplex.core.specs import DeclaredVariableSpec, StateSpec

tank = DeclaredElement('tank', variables={'x': DeclaredVariableSpec(10.0, StateSpec())})
decay = RateInteraction('decay', target='tank', state='x', rate=lambda s: -0.5 * s.target.x)
sorted(decay.get_tags().laws), sorted(decay.get_tags().time)
trajectory = simulate(CompositeSystem(elements=[tank], interactions=[decay]), until=2)
round(trajectory.get_values('tank.x')[-1], 2)

    (['rate'], ['continuous'])
3.68

    The decay has the law kind rate and the time continuous, and the tank falls from 10 to 3.68 after two time units.

    An incompatible interaction

    A MapInteraction returns an update law of discrete time. It cannot join the rate in one system unless the system has a time that joins the two, so simulate raises a TagError that names the tag the parts disagree on.

    from skcomplex.core.components.elements import DeclaredElement
from skcomplex.core.components.interactions import MapInteraction, RateInteraction
from skcomplex.core.components.systems import CompositeSystem
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import simulate
from skcomplex.core.errors import TagError
from skcomplex.core.specs import DeclaredVariableSpec, StateSpec

tank = DeclaredElement('tank', variables={'x': DeclaredVariableSpec(10.0, StateSpec())})
decay = RateInteraction('decay', target='tank', state='x', rate=lambda s: -0.5 * s.target.x)
halve = MapInteraction('halve', target='tank', state='x', function=lambda s: s.target.x / 2)
sorted(halve.get_tags().laws), sorted(halve.get_tags().time)
try:
    simulate(CompositeSystem(elements=[tank], interactions=[decay, halve]), until=1)
except TagError as error:
    message = str(error)
message
assert "tag 'time'" in message

    (['update'], ['discrete'])
The parts 'decay' and 'halve' disagree on the tag 'time': 'decay' has continuous and 'halve' has discrete. Give the system a time that joins them, or bind parts of one time.

    The map has the law kind update and the time discrete. The message says that decay and halve disagree on the tag time, and asks for a time that joins them or for parts of one time.

# group-cellular-automata Specification

## Purpose
Lets a user run cellular automata over finitely generated groups, given by finite presentations, Mealy automata, or
permutation or matrix generators, decide their injectivity and surjectivity on finite universes, study linear automata
and subshifts, and measure their information content.

## Requirements

### Requirement: Automata run on Cayley graphs of groups
`LocalRuleLaw` laws on site groups SHALL run under discrete time on group spaces, the Cayley graphs of finitely presented
groups built on finite quotients or on finite balls, with memory sets given as group elements. A group space SHALL be a
space of the lattice kind, so the solvers and backends that declare lattice support run it.

#### Scenario: Free group majority
- **WHEN** the majority automaton on the free group with two generators runs on a ball of radius 6
- **THEN** every interior cell takes the majority value of itself and its four neighbours given by the generators and
  their inverses

#### Scenario: Orientation is kept
- **WHEN** a rule copies the value at the generator `a` on the Heisenberg group
- **THEN** each cell takes the value of `g a`, not of `a g`

### Requirement: Group spaces declare what they offer
A group space SHALL declare the lattice options it does not offer, a regular geometry and an image layout, and
validation SHALL report every law, schedule, or visual that needs them, and a ball above the site limit, each as an
issue with its path and a fix, before any group is built.

#### Scenario: Unsupported schedule
- **WHEN** a sampling law with the checkerboard schedule runs on a group space
- **THEN** validation reports one issue whose path is the law and whose fix names the synchronous and random-sequential
  schedules

#### Scenario: Site limit
- **WHEN** a group space asks for a ball of the free group of rank 3 of radius 12
- **THEN** validation reports that the ball exceeds the site limit and names the largest radius that fits, without
  calling GAP

### Requirement: Named groups live in the automata systems
The core SHALL name no specific group, and SHALL take finitely presented, automaton, permutation, and matrix groups in
their general form, as native GAP or Sage groups.
Named groups, such as the free, Heisenberg, lamplighter, Baumslag-Solitar, and Grigorchuk groups, SHALL be realizations
in `skcomplex.systems.computer_science.automata`.

#### Scenario: Grigorchuk group from the library
- **WHEN** a user imports `grigorchuk_group` from `skcomplex.systems.computer_science.automata`
- **THEN** it returns an automaton group built by `libgap.AutomatonGroup`, and no public name of `skcomplex.core.components`
  names a specific group

#### Scenario: Grigorchuk group in the general form
- **WHEN** a user writes the Grigorchuk group with `libgap.AutomatonGroup` from its wreath recursion
- **THEN** `CayleySpace` takes it, and no field module of `skcomplex.systems` is imported

### Requirement: Groups are given by other presentations
Group spaces SHALL be built from automaton groups given by a Mealy automaton as a wreath recursion, and from permutation
or matrix groups given by generators. Their balls SHALL hold one site per distinct element, decided by the word problem
of the presentation, not by relators.

#### Scenario: Grigorchuk growth
- **WHEN** a user builds group spaces on balls of the Grigorchuk group with the generators `a, b, c, d` and radii 0 to 5
- **THEN** the balls hold 1, 5, 11, 23, 40, and 68 sites, and every site is a distinct element of the group

#### Scenario: Free matrix group
- **WHEN** a user builds balls of radii 0 to 3 of the matrix group generated by `[[1, 2], [0, 1]]` and
  `[[1, 0], [2, 1]]` over the integers
- **THEN** the balls hold 1, 5, 17, and 53 sites, as in the free group of rank 2

#### Scenario: Finite permutation group
- **WHEN** a user builds balls of the symmetric group on three points generated by `(0 1)` and `(0 1 2)`
- **THEN** the ball of radius 1 holds 4 sites, and the balls of radius 2 and more hold all 6 elements

#### Scenario: Missing extra
- **WHEN** a user builds a group space of an automaton group without the `groups` extra installed
- **THEN** an `ImportError` is raised whose message names the `groups` extra and how to install it

#### Scenario: Rules run without GAP
- **WHEN** a rule runs on a built group space for many steps
- **THEN** each step reads the neighbour table on the NumPy or Numba backend and makes no call to GAP

### Requirement: Automaton groups give level quotients
Automaton groups SHALL give finite quotients by their action on a level of the tree, on which configurations are
periodic and the theory checks apply.

#### Scenario: Grigorchuk level quotients
- **WHEN** a user builds group spaces on the quotients of the Grigorchuk group by its action on levels 1 to 4 of the
  binary tree
- **THEN** they hold 2, 8, 128, and 4096 sites, and a rule runs on them with periodic configurations

### Requirement: Group spaces stream as Cayley graphs
Group spaces SHALL stream to Rerun as graph nodes and edges, with one node per site, one edge per generator, a layout
computed once, and node colours from the cell states at each step.

#### Scenario: Grigorchuk ball in the viewer
- **WHEN** the majority automaton on a ball of radius 8 of the Grigorchuk group is logged
- **THEN** the viewer shows 271 nodes on rings by word length, joined by the generator edges, and their colours change
  with the states over time

### Requirement: Injectivity and surjectivity are decided on finite universes
`automaton` SHALL take a `System` with a `LocalRuleInteraction` as its source, and SHALL decide injectivity, surjectivity,
and pre-injectivity of the automaton on finite quotients and
windows, find Garden of Eden patterns, and find the inverse of a reversible automaton.

#### Scenario: Life has a Garden of Eden
- **WHEN** a user checks the Game of Life on a 6 × 6 torus
- **THEN** it is reported not surjective, with a configuration that has no preimage

#### Scenario: Reversible rule
- **WHEN** a user asks for the inverse of the elementary shift automaton
- **THEN** the result is the shift in the opposite direction, and their composition is the identity

### Requirement: Linear automata are decided algebraically
A linear automaton SHALL be a `LocalRuleLaw` whose rule is a `LinearRuleSpec`, a group-ring element whose invertibility and
surjectivity `automaton` decides algebraically, with its matrix and its Laurent polynomial as the kinds `'matrix'` and
`'polynomial'`.

#### Scenario: Laplacian over a finite field
- **WHEN** a user checks the discrete Laplacian on the cycle of length 5 over the field with 2 elements
- **THEN** the answer matches the invertibility of the corresponding circulant matrix

### Requirement: Subshifts constrain configurations
A subshift of finite type SHALL be a `SubshiftSpec`, the domain of a state. The kinds `'admissible'`, `'violations'`, `'count'`,
and `'sample'` of `automaton` SHALL check configurations against forbidden patterns, count admissible patterns, and
sample admissible configurations. A run SHALL check that every cell holds a value of the alphabet, and SHALL NOT enforce
admissibility.

#### Scenario: Golden mean shift
- **WHEN** a user counts admissible words of length n in the subshift that forbids `11`
- **THEN** the counts follow the Fibonacci numbers, and the entropy estimate approaches the log of the golden ratio

#### Scenario: A run does not enforce admissibility
- **WHEN** a system whose state has the domain of the subshift that forbids `11` runs from a configuration that holds `11`
- **THEN** the run completes, and the kind `'admissible'` reports that configuration as not admissible

### Requirement: Automata information is measured
The analysis layer SHALL estimate the entropy of configuration sets along windows, track the spatial entropy rate of
runs, and compute difference patterns of single-cell perturbations.

#### Scenario: Entropy decreases
- **WHEN** an elementary automaton runs from a random configuration on a large ring
- **THEN** the spatial entropy rate never increases from one step to the next beyond estimation error

### Requirement: A cellular automaton is a system
A cellular automaton SHALL be a `System` with a `LocalRuleInteraction`, and no class SHALL duplicate it. The `run` of a
`LocalRuleLaw` SHALL be one step of the map on configurations, and several steps SHALL be a loop over that `run` or a
`simulate` run of the system.

#### Scenario: A step and a run agree
- **WHEN** a user applies the `run` of the `LocalRuleLaw` of a shift twice to a configuration, and simulates the system
  of the same rule from that configuration until 2
- **THEN** the last frame of the simulation equals the result of the loop

#### Scenario: No class for an automaton
- **WHEN** a user lists the public names of `skcomplex`
- **THEN** none is `Automaton`, `LinearAutomaton`, or `Subshift`

### Requirement: Local rules read at state-dependent group elements
A local rule on a group space SHALL read, at every site `g`, the cells at the products `g x(g) s` for the words `s` of
a memory set, where `x(g)` is the group element that the first read state holds as the number of its site. On a finite
group every product SHALL be a site. On a ball a product outside it, or reached through a site outside it, SHALL read
the fill value of the space, as SHALL a value that names no site. A rule over such a memory SHALL run on the
synchronous and random schedules, and SHALL be rejected with a named error on the checkerboard and colored schedules
and when it asks for degrees. A space that is no group space SHALL reject such a memory with a named error.

#### Scenario: The self-indexed shift on a finite group
- **WHEN** the rule `τ(x)(g) = x(g · x(g))` runs one step on the symmetric group of degree 3
- **THEN** every cell takes the value of the cell at the product of its site and its value in the group

#### Scenario: Products leave a ball
- **WHEN** the same rule runs on a ball of the free group
- **THEN** a cell whose product lies outside the ball takes the fill value

#### Scenario: Equivariance on a finite group
- **WHEN** a configuration is translated by a group element before one step on a finite group
- **THEN** the image is the translate of the image of the configuration

### Requirement: States take values in truncated power series rings
A state SHALL take values in the ring `K[[t]]` of formal power series over the rationals, the reals, or a prime field,
truncated at a declared order, holding every series by its coefficients as components. The ring SHALL multiply series
by the Cauchy product truncated at the order, reduce coefficients modulo the characteristic, and convert coefficients to
and from Sage's power series ring of the same precision. An order that is not a positive integer or an unknown field
SHALL raise a named error.

#### Scenario: Truncated product
- **WHEN** `1 + t` and `1 - t` are multiplied in the ring truncated at order 4
- **THEN** the coefficients of the product are `1, 0, -1, 0`

#### Scenario: Coefficients round-trip through Sage
- **WHEN** coefficients are converted to a Sage power series and back
- **THEN** they are unchanged

### Requirement: Local rules update states of several components
A function local rule SHALL read and set states of several components per cell, receiving one row of components per
cell for every neighbour, and returning one row per cell. Table and totalistic rules SHALL reject such states with a
named error.

#### Scenario: Power series automaton
- **WHEN** the automaton `τ(x)(n) = x(n) - t x(n + 1)` over the rationals runs three steps from a single 1
- **THEN** the cells hold the coefficients of `(1 - t σ)^3` applied to it, the binomial coefficients with alternating
  signs at the matching powers of `t`

### Requirement: The CC and EX automata are catalog systems
The catalog SHALL include, in `computer_science/automata`, the models of Ceccherini-Silberstein and Coornaert (2023)
and of its exercises book that the reference audit lists as missing: the self-indexed shift (Example 1.8.2), the power
series automaton (Exercise 1.37), the subtraction automaton over the naturals (Example 3.3.3), Muller's pre-injective
but not surjective automaton on F2 (Sections 5.11 and 8.10), the surjective but not pre-injective linear automaton on F2
(Section 8.11), Life in a tree on F4 (Exercise 5.14), the nilpotent automaton of class n (Exercise 1.15), the
involutive automaton (Exercise 1.36), the group-valued difference automaton (Exercise 3.24), the real quadratic
automaton (Exercise 5.64), the even and S-gap subshifts (Exercises 1.85 and 1.92), the Morse subshift (Exercises 3.51
and 3.52), the context-free subshift (Exercise 6.80), and the adding machine (Exercise 6.59). Each SHALL have tests, an
inventory entry citing its section, and a gallery example.

#### Scenario: Muller's automaton is not surjective
- **WHEN** Muller's automaton runs one step
- **THEN** the second coordinate of every cell is 0, and a nonzero configuration of finite support inside the ball has
  a nonzero image

#### Scenario: The surjective linear automaton is not pre-injective
- **WHEN** the surjective linear automaton runs one step from `(0, 1)` at the identity and 0 elsewhere
- **THEN** every cell is 0

#### Scenario: Nilpotency and involution
- **WHEN** the nilpotent automaton of class n runs n steps from any configuration of a ring, and the involutive
  automaton runs two steps
- **THEN** the first ends at the zero configuration, which n - 1 steps do not always reach, and the second returns to
  its start

#### Scenario: Subshift samplers stay admissible
- **WHEN** the S-gap and the context-free subshift samplers run
- **THEN** every configuration they visit satisfies the constraint of its subshift

#### Scenario: The adding machine counts
- **WHEN** the adding machine on `L` digits in base `q` runs `q^L` steps
- **THEN** its digits read every residue modulo `q^L` once, in order, and return to the start

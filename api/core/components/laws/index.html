    Laws

    Define laws, the rules by which states change.

    A law is one equation or rule for the states it targets, such as a rate, the next value at a step, a random jump, or an algebraic equation. Elements, interactions, and systems return laws, and the compiler lowers them to a problem.

    ArrayUpdateLaw(target, function, *, label='', score=None)

    Bases: BaseLaw

    A law that replaces a whole array state by the output of a function.

    At every step the function receives the state of every member as one array, shaped like the lattice or with one row per member of a group on the edges or hyperedges of a network. A state of several components per member has its components on a last axis.     BilinearLaw(target, first, second, rate, outcomes=None, *, conserved=False, label='')

    Bases: BaseLaw

    A law where members of two distributions meet and change class.

    A distribution is a group of elements, one per class of a trait, that holds how much of a population sits in each class. When an amount of the first distribution f in class h meets an amount of the second distribution g in class k, at the rate rate[h, k], it adds to class i of the target with the probability outcomes[h, k, i]. The law adds sum_hk rate[h, k] outcomes[h, k, i] f_h g_k to the rate of the target at i. A positive rate gives a gain that leaves the first distribution in place, and a negative rate with the first distribution as target gives a loss.

    A conserved law also takes the matching amount from class h of the first distribution, so the total stays the same: df_i/dt = sum_hk rate[h, k] outcomes[h, k, i] f_h g_k - f_i sum_k rate[i, k] g_k. A second distribution that no law changes is a fixed input, and a conserved law without one is a linear redistribution between classes.

    Unlike RateLaw, which gives the rate of one state by an expression, it sums a product of two distributions over every pair of classes. Choose it when the change of a population comes from binary meetings of its members.

    It appears in many fields. Traders exchange wealth when they meet, people shift their opinion after a conversation, immune cells destroy tumour cells they meet, and gas molecules change velocity when they collide.

    CascadeLaw(drive, relax, stable, max_iterations=100000, activity=None, *, label='')

    Bases: BaseLaw

    A law that drives a lattice once per step and relaxes it until it is stable.

    The drive laws run once per step. Then the relax laws run again and again until stable holds, which is one cascade, or avalanche. Each cascade that changes the lattice is recorded as an event with its size, the number of site updates, its duration, the number of iterations, and its area, the number of distinct sites updated. A site update is a site that changes in an iteration, or a site that activity marks as active before it, such as a toppling site.

    Unlike UpdateLaw, which runs once per step, it runs its relax laws until the lattice settles, so one step holds a whole avalanche. Choose it when a slow drive and fast relaxation are separated in time, as in self-organized criticality.

    It appears in many fields. Grains topple in the sandpile model of Bak, Tang, and Wiesenfeld, slips spread along a fault in the earthquake model of Olami, Feder, and Christensen, and trees burn in a forest fire model.

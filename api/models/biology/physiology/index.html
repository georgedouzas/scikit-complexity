    Physiology

    Physiology: delayed feedback, excitable tissue, the circulation, cell exchange, regulation, and adaptivity.

    AdaptiveResponderElement(label, visual=None, *, e=0.0, c=0.2, S=1.0, response_cost=0.0, restore_cost=0.0, repair_cost=0.0, prepare_cost=0.0, eta=0.5, anticipation=0.0, c0=0.2, tau=50.0, k0=0.1, kappa=0.2, price=1.0, theta=1.0, repair=5.0, upkeep=0.05, height=1.0, width=2.0, period=10.0, start=1.0)

    Bases: Element

    System that counters episodes of demand on a critical parameter and pays for its response in four costs.

    The excursion of the critical parameter follows de/dt = (1 - a) s(t) - c e - k0 e, where the stimulus s is a train of episodes of height height and width width every period from start, c e is the response of a mechanism of capacity c, and a is the share of every episode that anticipation avoids. The capacity grows with the demand and decays to its baseline, dc/dt = eta e^2 - (c - c0) / tau, and the response draws the reserves, which are restored, dS/dt = -c e + kappa (1 - S). The four costs of Mobus accumulate as stocks: the response cost is the response effort c e, the restore cost the work price kappa (1 - S) of refilling the reserves, the repair cost repair (e - theta) while the excursion exceeds the damage threshold theta, and the preparedness cost the upkeep upkeep (c - c0 + a) of the extra capacity and of the anticipation.

    The element holds the states and parameters and has no laws of its own: the interactions of adaptive_response_costs read its parameters.

    references=('Mobus 2022', 'Mobus 1999'),

def adaptive_response_costs(
    strategy: str = 'adaptive',
    *,
    height: float = 1.0,
    width: float = 2.0,
    period: float = 10.0,
) -> System:
    """Return a system that meets recurring demands by stimulus-response, adaptive response, or anticipation.

    Episodes of demand push a critical parameter away from its norm, and the system counters them, after Mobus. A
    stimulus-response keeps its baseline capacity, so large episodes overshoot the damage threshold and cost repairs.
    An adaptive response builds capacity under repeated demand and loses it when demand stops, at the price of its
    upkeep. An anticipatory response also predicts every episode and avoids half of it. Mobus's objective is the sum
    of the response, restore, and repair costs over the episodes, with the preparedness cost amortized over them:
    adaptive response lowers it below stimulus-response by avoiding repairs, and anticipation lowers it further by
    cutting the response it needs.

    Args:
        strategy:
            `'stimulus-response'`, `'adaptive'`, or `'anticipatory'`.

        height:
            The height of the episodes of demand.

        width:
            The duration of the episodes.

        period:
            The time between the starts of episodes.

    Returns:
        system:
            The model, with the excursion `organism.e`, the capacity `organism.c`, the reserves `organism.S`, and the
            costs `organism.response_cost`, `organism.restore_cost`, `organism.repair_cost`, and
            `organism.prepare_cost`.

    Raises:
        ValidationError:
            The strategy is unknown.

    Examples:
        >>> from skcomplex.models.biology.physiology import adaptive_response_costs
        >>> adaptive_response_costs('stimulus-response').elements[0].eta
        0.0
    """
    if strategy not in STRATEGIES:
        error_msg = f'Unknown strategy {strategy!r}. Use one of {", ".join(STRATEGIES)}.'
        raise ValidationError(error_msg)
    eta, anticipation = STRATEGIES[strategy]
    organism = AdaptiveResponderElement(
        'organism',
        eta=eta,
        anticipation=anticipation,
        height=height,
        width=width,
        period=period,
    )
    interactions = [
        RateInteraction(
            'stimulus',
            target='organism',
            state='e',
            rate=lambda s: (1 - s.target.anticipation)
            * pulse_train(s.t, organism.period, organism.width, organism.height, start=organism.start),
        ),
        ReactionInteraction(
            'effort',
            target='organism',
            reactants={'e': 1, 'S': 1},
            products={'response_cost': 1},
            propensity=lambda s: s.target.c * s.target.e,
        ),
        ReactionInteraction(
            'effort_decay',
            target='organism',
            reactants={'e': 1},
            propensity=lambda s: s.target.k0 * s.target.e,
        ),
        RateInteraction('capacity_growth', target='organism', state='c', rate=lambda s: s.target.eta * s.target.e**2),
        RelaxationInteraction(
            'capacity_relaxation',
            target='organism',
            state='c',
            goal=lambda s: s.target.c0,
            time=lambda s: s.target.tau,
        ),
        RateInteraction('restoration', target='organism', state='S', rate=lambda s: s.target.kappa * (1 - s.target.S)),
        RateInteraction(
            'restoration_cost',
            target='organism',
            state='restore_cost',
            rate=lambda s: s.target.price * s.target.kappa * (1 - s.target.S),
        ),
        RateInteraction(
            'repair_cost',
            target='organism',
            state='repair_cost',
            rate=lambda s: s.target.repair * where(s.target.e > s.target.theta, s.target.e - s.target.theta, 0),
        ),
        RateInteraction(
            'preparation_cost',
            target='organism',
            state='prepare_cost',
            rate=lambda s: s.target.upkeep * (s.target.c - s.target.c0 + s.target.anticipation),
        ),
    ]
    return System(elements=[organism], interactions=interactions)

def glucose_dual_hormone_control(
    *,
    glucagon: bool = True,
    meal: float = 1.0,
    exercise: float = 0.8,
    meal_time: float = 2.0,
    exercise_time: float = 12.0,
    duration: float = 2.0,
) -> System:
    """Return blood glucose stabilised by antagonistic hormone loops, insulin against highs and glucagon against lows.

    A meal raises the glucose and secretes insulin, which raises the uptake of glucose, and an exercise lowers it and
    secretes glucagon, which releases glucose from the liver. Each receptor acts in one direction only, so one loop
    alone corrects excursions to one side of the norm. Two opposing loops on the same quantity attenuate the
    excursions to both sides, after Konieczny et al. The time unit is about one hour, and the glucose is relative to
    its norm.

    Args:
        glucagon:
            Whether the glucagon loop acts, `False` for insulin alone.

        meal:
            The rate of glucose intake during the meal.

        exercise:
            The rate of extra glucose uptake during the exercise.

        meal_time:
            The start of the meal.

        exercise_time:
            The start of the exercise.

        duration:
            The duration of the meal and of the exercise.

    Returns:
        system:
            The model, with the glucose `organism.G`, the insulin `organism.I`, and the glucagon `organism.N`.

    Examples:
        >>> from skcomplex.models.biology.physiology import glucose_dual_hormone_control
        >>> glucose_dual_hormone_control(glucagon=False).elements[0].k_n
        0.0
    """
    k_n = 1.0 if glucagon else 0.0
    insulin, glucagon_level = 2.0 * 0.5, 1.0 * 0.5
    organism = GlucoseControlElement(
        'organism',
        I=insulin,
        N=glucagon_level,
        R=(0.5 + 0.5 * insulin) - k_n * glucagon_level,
        k_n=k_n,
        meal=meal,
        exercise=exercise,
        meal_time=meal_time,
        exercise_time=exercise_time,
        duration=duration,
    )
    interactions = [
        ReactionInteraction('release', target='organism', products={'G': 1}, propensity=lambda s: s.target.R),
        ReactionInteraction(
            'basal_uptake',
            target='organism',
            reactants={'G': 1},
            propensity=lambda s: s.target.k0 * s.target.G,
        ),
        ReactionInteraction(
            'insulin_uptake',
            target='organism',
            reactants={'G': 1},
            modifiers={'I': 1},
            propensity=lambda s: s.target.k_i * s.target.I * s.target.G,
        ),
        ReactionInteraction(
            'glucagon_release',
            target='organism',
            products={'G': 1},
            propensity=lambda s: s.target.k_n * s.target.N,
        ),
        RateInteraction(
            'meal',
            target='organism',
            state='G',
            rate=lambda s: s.target.meal * pulse(s.t, organism.meal_time, organism.duration),
        ),
        RateInteraction(
            'exercise',
            target='organism',
            state='G',
            rate=lambda s: -s.target.exercise * pulse(s.t, organism.exercise_time, organism.duration),
        ),
        ReactionInteraction(
            'insulin_secretion',
            target='organism',
            products={'I': 1},
            propensity=lambda s: s.target.s_i * hill(s.target.G, s.target.K, s.target.n),
        ),
        ReactionInteraction(
            'insulin_clearance',
            target='organism',
            reactants={'I': 1},
            propensity=lambda s: s.target.d_i * s.target.I,
        ),
        ReactionInteraction(
            'glucagon_secretion',
            target='organism',
            products={'N': 1},
            propensity=lambda s: s.target.s_n * (1 - hill(s.target.G, s.target.K, s.target.n)),
        ),
        ReactionInteraction(
            'glucagon_clearance',
            target='organism',
            reactants={'N': 1},
            propensity=lambda s: s.target.d_n * s.target.N,
        ),
    ]
    return System(elements=[organism], interactions=interactions)

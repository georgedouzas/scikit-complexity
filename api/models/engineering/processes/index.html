    cascade_classifier(n_stages=10, *, k=0.6, feed_stage=None, feed=1.0)

    Return a cascade classifier, a column of stages that separates a narrow size class of particles by an air flow.

    Material leaves every stage at unit rate, a fraction k upwards and 1 - k downwards. The top stage releases into the fine product and the bottom stage into the coarse product. Material of one size class is fed into one stage at a constant rate. At steady state the share of the feed that ends in the fine product is F_f = (1 - ((1 - k) / k)^(z + 1 - i)) / (1 - ((1 - k) / k)^(z + 1)) for z stages and feeding at stage i counted from the top, Barsky's fractional extraction.

    Parameters:

    Name Type Description Default
    n_stages int

    The number of stages z.

    		 10
    k float

    The distribution coefficient, the fraction of material moving up from a stage.

    		 0.6
    feed_stage int | None

    The stage that receives the feed, counted from 1 at the top, or None for the middle stage.

    		 None
    feed float

    The feed rate.

    		 1.0

    Returns:

    Name Type Description
    system System

    The model, with the material stage_<i>.m of each stage from the top, and the accumulated products fine.m and coarse.m.

    Examples:

    >>> from skcomplex.models.engineering.processes import cascade_classifier
>>> len(cascade_classifier(n_stages=4).interactions)

def cascade_classifier(
    n_stages: int = 10,
    *,
    k: float = 0.6,
    feed_stage: int | None = None,
    feed: float = 1.0,
) -> System:
    """Return a cascade classifier, a column of stages that separates a narrow size class of particles by an air flow.

    Material leaves every stage at unit rate, a fraction `k` upwards and `1 - k` downwards. The top stage releases
    into the fine product and the bottom stage into the coarse product. Material of one size class is fed into one
    stage at a constant rate. At steady state the share of the feed that ends in the fine product is
    `F_f = (1 - ((1 - k) / k)^(z + 1 - i)) / (1 - ((1 - k) / k)^(z + 1))` for `z` stages and feeding at stage `i`
    counted from the top, Barsky's fractional extraction.

    Args:
        n_stages:
            The number of stages `z`.

        k:
            The distribution coefficient, the fraction of material moving up from a stage.

        feed_stage:
            The stage that receives the feed, counted from 1 at the top, or `None` for the middle stage.

        feed:
            The feed rate.

    Returns:
        system:
            The model, with the material `stage_<i>.m` of each stage from the top, and the accumulated products
            `fine.m` and `coarse.m`.

    Examples:
        >>> from skcomplex.models.engineering.processes import cascade_classifier
        >>> len(cascade_classifier(n_stages=4).interactions)
        8
    """
    entry = (n_stages + 1) // 2 if feed_stage is None else feed_stage
    stages = [StageElement(f'stage_{index}', feed=feed if index == entry else 0.0) for index in range(1, n_stages + 1)]
    labels = ['fine', *(stage.label for stage in stages), 'coarse']
    interactions = [
        TransferInteraction(
            f'up_{index}',
            source=labels[index],
            target=labels[index - 1],
            state='m',
            amount=lambda s: k * s.source.m,
        )
        for index in range(1, n_stages + 1)
    ] + [
        TransferInteraction(
            f'down_{index}',
            source=labels[index],
            target=labels[index + 1],
            state='m',
            amount=lambda s: (1 - k) * s.source.m,
        )
        for index in range(1, n_stages + 1)
    ]
    return System(elements=[StageElement('fine'), *stages, StageElement('coarse')], interactions=interactions)

    food_relief(affected=100000.0, *, ration=0.0005, onset=10.0, shipping_delay=14.0, cover=7.0, stock_time=7.0)

    Return a food relief system, a pipeline that ships food to a warehouse and distributes it to people in need.

    A disaster at onset leaves affected people who need a daily ration. The relief agency ships food at the need plus corrections that close the gaps between the warehouse stock and its desired cover of the need, and between the food in transit and the need over the shipping delay. Shipped food enters the pipeline and arrives at the warehouse through a third-order material delay. The warehouse distributes the need while its stock lasts. Food is conserved, so almost nothing reaches the people in the first days after the disaster, and the gap closes as the pipeline fills. The structure follows the relief food example of Bala, Arshad and Noh (2017, Section 4.11), with illustrative parameters in tonnes and days.

    Parameters:

    Name Type Description Default
    affected float

    The number of people in need after the disaster.

    		 100000.0
    ration float

    The daily ration in tonnes per person.

    		 0.0005
    onset float

    The day of the disaster.

    		 10.0
    shipping_delay float

    The average time from shipping to arrival, in days.

    		 14.0
    cover float

    The desired warehouse stock in days of need.

    		 7.0
    stock_time float

    The time to correct the warehouse stock, in days.

    		 7.0

    Returns:

    Name Type Description
    system System

    The model, with the food stocks relief.in_transit and relief.warehouse in tonnes, and the flows relief.shipping, relief.arrivals, and relief.distribution in tonnes per day, over days. The observables of the model are relief.need and relief.shipments.

    irrigation(*, river_inflow=0.5, seasonality=0.9, reservoir_capacity=80.0, potential_et=0.4, canal_loss=0.2, pump_capacity=0.2)

    Return an irrigation system, a reservoir and an aquifer that keep the root zone of the crops supplied.

    A river fills the reservoir with a yearly cycle, and the reservoir loses water to evaporation and spills above its capacity. The irrigation demand is the potential crop evapotranspiration plus the refill of the root zone towards a target fraction of its field capacity. The reservoir releases the demand grossed up for the canal losses, as far as its stock allows, and the losses seep into the aquifer. When the release falls short, the farmers pump the unmet demand from the aquifer up to the pump capacity. The crops draw water from the root zone at the potential rate scaled by a water-stress table, and the root zone percolates to the aquifer, which drains as baseflow. Water is conserved, so in the dry season the reservoir falls, the pumping rises, and the aquifer is drawn down. The structure follows the irrigation example of Bala, Arshad and Noh (2017, Section 4.11), with illustrative parameters in millions of cubic metres and days.

    Parameters:

    Name Type Description Default
    river_inflow float

    The mean river inflow to the reservoir, per day.

    		 0.5
    seasonality float

    The relative amplitude of the yearly inflow cycle.

    		 0.9
    reservoir_capacity float

    The capacity of the reservoir.

    		 80.0
    potential_et float

    The crop water requirement, the potential evapotranspiration of the irrigated area per day.

    		 0.4
    canal_loss float

    The fraction of the release lost from the canals to the aquifer.

    		 0.2
    pump_capacity float

    The largest groundwater pumping per day.

    		 0.2

    Returns:

    Name Type Description
    system System

    The model, with the water stocks scheme.reservoir, scheme.soil, and scheme.groundwater, and the flows scheme.inflow, scheme.evaporation, scheme.spill, scheme.irrigation, scheme.seepage, scheme.groundwater_pumping, scheme.crop_water, scheme.percolation, and scheme.baseflow per day, over days. The observables of the model are scheme.demand, scheme.release, and scheme.pumping.

    rice_milling(*, harvest=2000.0, seasonality=0.8, procurement_delay=1.0, outturn=0.65, capacity=2000.0, expansion_time=12.0)

    Return a rice milling system, a mill that buys paddy, mills it into rice and by-products, and sells them.

    Paddy arrives from the farms through a third-order procurement delay, at a rate that follows the harvest season around its mean. The mill processes its paddy stock in a month, as far as its milling capacity allows, and splits the milled paddy by mass into rice, the outturn, husk, bran, and milling losses. Rice, husk, and bran leave their inventories for the market at a fixed coverage. The capacity adjusts to the smoothed indicated milling rate, so in the harvest season the capacity lags and paddy accumulates, and in the lean season the capacity idles. Grain is conserved, every change of the paddy, rice, husk, and bran stocks is a flow, and over a year the milled paddy and the rice sales match the mean harvest and its outturn. The structure follows the rice milling case of Bala, Arshad and Noh (2017), with illustrative parameters in tonnes and months, since the case data are specific to their study.

    Parameters:

    Name Type Description Default
    harvest float

    The mean paddy procurement in tonnes per month.

    		 2000.0
    seasonality float

    The relative amplitude of the yearly harvest cycle.

    		 0.8
    procurement_delay float

    The average time for purchased paddy to reach the mill, in months.

    		 1.0
    outturn float

    The fraction of the milled paddy that becomes rice.

    		 0.65
    capacity float

    The initial milling capacity in tonnes of paddy per month.

    		 2000.0
    expansion_time float

    The time for the capacity to adjust to the perceived milling rate, in months.

    		 12.0

    Returns:

    Name Type Description
    system System

    The model, with the grain stocks mill.paddy, mill.rice, mill.husk, and mill.bran in tonnes, the capacity mill.capacity, and the flows mill.paddy_purchases, mill.milled_rice, mill.husk_output, mill.bran_output, mill.milling_losses, mill.rice_sales, mill.husk_sales, and mill.bran_sales in tonnes per month, over months. The observables of the model are mill.procurement, mill.indicated_milling, and mill.milling.

    Examples:

    >>> from skcomplex.models.engineering.processes import rice_milling
>>> rice_milling(capacity=1500.0).elements[0].capacity
1500.0

    Paddy arrives from the farms through a third-order procurement delay, at a rate that follows the harvest season
    around its mean. The mill processes its paddy stock in a month, as far as its milling capacity allows, and splits
    the milled paddy by mass into rice, the outturn, husk, bran, and milling losses. Rice, husk, and bran leave their
    inventories for the market at a fixed coverage. The capacity adjusts to the smoothed indicated milling rate, so in
    the harvest season the capacity lags and paddy accumulates, and in the lean season the capacity idles. Grain is
    conserved, every change of the paddy, rice, husk, and bran stocks is a flow, and over a year the milled paddy and
    the rice sales match the mean harvest and its outturn. The structure follows the rice milling case of Bala,
    Arshad and Noh (2017), with illustrative parameters in tonnes and months, since the case data are specific to
    their study.

    Args:
        harvest:
            The mean paddy procurement in tonnes per month.

        seasonality:
            The relative amplitude of the yearly harvest cycle.

        procurement_delay:
            The average time for purchased paddy to reach the mill, in months.

        outturn:
            The fraction of the milled paddy that becomes rice.

        capacity:
            The initial milling capacity in tonnes of paddy per month.

        expansion_time:
            The time for the capacity to adjust to the perceived milling rate, in months.

    Returns:
        system:
            The model, with the grain stocks `mill.paddy`, `mill.rice`, `mill.husk`, and `mill.bran` in tonnes, the
            capacity `mill.capacity`, and the flows `mill.paddy_purchases`, `mill.milled_rice`, `mill.husk_output`,
            `mill.bran_output`, `mill.milling_losses`, `mill.rice_sales`, `mill.husk_sales`, and `mill.bran_sales` in
            tonnes per month, over months. The observables of the model are `mill.procurement`,
            `mill.indicated_milling`, and `mill.milling`.

    Examples:
        >>> from skcomplex.models.engineering.processes import rice_milling
        >>> rice_milling(capacity=1500.0).elements[0].capacity
        1500.0
    """
    mill = RiceMillElement(
        'mill',
        paddy=harvest,
        rice=outturn * harvest,
        husk=HUSK_SHARE * harvest,
        bran=BRAN_SHARE * harvest,
        capacity=capacity,
        harvest=harvest,
        seasonality=seasonality,
        procurement_delay=procurement_delay,
        outturn=outturn,
        expansion_time=expansion_time,
    )
    return System(elements=[mill])

    solid_waste(population=1000000.0, *, growth_rate=0.02, per_capita=0.2, recycling_share=0.2, composting_share=0.3, landfill_capacity=2000000.0)

    Bases: BaseSystem

    The kinetic system of criminality in a society.

    It includes the security forces that act against it. Citizens are a distribution over criminality classes and security agents a distribution over efficacy classes, held by site groups on lattices of the classes. Encounters between citizens spread crime when it attracts more than honesty redeems, encounters with security agents move criminal citizens toward honesty, a micro-scale action, and the security forces grow in proportion to the mean criminality, a macro-scale action that reads a moment of the citizens. The security forces grow while crime is high, crime recedes, and the forces retire, so crime and security oscillate toward a balance where some crime persists.

    The system has the distributions citizens and security and the interactions contagion, deterrence, and recruitment. Its standard observables are the mean criminality, the criminal fraction, and the density of the security forces.

    EvacuationSystem(branching=2, depth=2, speeds=3, *, mobility=0.5, crowding=2.0, recovery=0.5, label='system')

    Bases: BaseSystem

    The kinetic system of the evacuation of a building whose rooms form a network.

    The crowd is a distribution over pairs of a room and a speed class, held by a site group on a two-dimensional lattice indexed by room and speed, with one last row of sites for the outside. The rooms form a balanced tree with the exit in the root room, and the walking encounters read its doors, so pedestrians walk to the next room on the way out at a rate proportional to their speed. Encounters within a room slow pedestrians down, and the exit signs speed them up again, so the crowd queues in the rooms near the exit and the evacuated fraction grows along an S-shaped curve.

    The rooms are the nodes and the speed classes the discrete activity of the kinetic structure for a network of Bellomo et al. (2017, sections 3.4 and 3.4.1), and the slowing and guiding encounters follow the behavioural crowd of Bellomo and Gibelli (2015). The crowd of chapter 6 of Bellomo et al. (2017), which moves in continuous space around walls and obstacles, is a different model. The system has the crowd crowd, the exit signs signs, and the interactions walking, crowding, and guidance. Its standard observables are the evacuated fraction and the mean walking speed.

    KineticCrowdInteraction(label, *, target, length=20.0, width=5.0, cell=2.5, packing=4.0, quality=1.0, stress=0.5, deceleration=0.95, rate=1.0, wall_distance=0.5, dt=0.1, panic_time=None, panic_stress=0.9, band_width=1.0)

    Bases: Interaction

    Decision process of Bellomo's kinetic crowd model.

    A direct simulation Monte Carlo of the walkers runs it. The density rho and the mean velocity xi are counted on square cells of side cell of a street of length length, periodic along it, and width width, between walls, with rho = 1 at packing walkers per unit area. Every walker interacts with the crowd with the probability eta_A dt in a step of length dt. It first turns to the preferred direction nu_p ~ rho nu_v + (1 - rho) (beta nu_s + (1 - beta) nu_t) / |.|, where nu_v points down the gradient of the density, nu_s along the mean velocity, and nu_t to its target, and then adjusts its speed to the perceived density rho_theta = rho + d / sqrt(1 + d^2) ((1 - rho) H(d) + rho H(-d)) of the derivative d of the density along its new direction: a walker no faster than the mean speed accelerates to xi + alpha beta (1 - rho_theta) (alpha beta - xi) with the probability alpha beta (1 - rho_theta), and a faster one decelerates to xi - rho_theta xi with the probability (1 - c alpha beta) rho_theta. A walker at a distance d < d_w from a wall and heading to it then reduces the normal component of its velocity to d / d_w of it at constant speed, and every walker moves along its velocity. From the time panic_time on, the stress parameter is panic_stress in place of stress. The interaction reports the band index, the mean of |rho_1 - rho_2| / (rho_1 + rho_2) over the cells of the street where walkers are, of the densities smoothed by a Gaussian of width band_width, and the mean speed.

    def walk(self, system: SystemView) -> None:
    """Run the decisions, the turns at the walls, and the motion of one step.

    Args:
        system:
            The system.
    """
    views = system.agents(self.target, KineticWalkerView)
    if not views:
        return
    walkers = {
        name: np.array([getattr(view, name) for view in views])
        for name in ('x', 'y', 'speed', 'direction', 'target')
    }
    stress = self.stress
    if self.panic_time is not None and system.time >= self.panic_time:
        stress = self.panic_stress
    velocity = np.stack([np.cos(walkers['direction']), np.sin(walkers['direction'])], axis=1)
    fields = self._get_fields(walkers['x'], walkers['y'], walkers['speed'][:, None] * velocity, walkers['target'])
    self._interact(walkers, fields, stress, system.rng)
    self._avoid_walls(walkers)
    walkers['x'] = np.mod(walkers['x'] + self.dt * walkers['speed'] * np.cos(walkers['direction']), self.length)
    walkers['y'] = np.clip(
        walkers['y'] + self.dt * walkers['speed'] * np.sin(walkers['direction']),
        0.0,
        self.width,
    )
    for position, view in enumerate(views):
        for name in ('x', 'y', 'speed', 'direction'):
            setattr(view, name, float(walkers[name][position]))
    system.report('band_index', self._get_band_index(fields['first'], fields['second']))
    system.report('mean_speed', float(walkers['speed'].mean()))

    Bellomo's kinetic crowd of two groups walking in opposite directions.

    They walk along a street with walls. Walkers in a continuous venue choose first a direction and then a speed (Bellomo et al. 2017 sections 6.4 and 6.5). The preferred direction weighs the search of less crowded areas, the attraction of the mean stream, and the target, by the density rho and the stress beta (Eq. 6.19). The speed then adjusts to the density perceived along that direction, with the venue quality alpha and the stress (Eqs. 6.22 to 6.24), and walkers near a wall turn parallel to it at constant speed (Eq. 6.25). Panic raises the stress from beta_1 to beta_2 at a critical time (Eqs. 6.27 and 6.28). The kinetic equation is solved, as in the book, by a particle method close to direct simulation Monte Carlo, here with one particle per walker. In space-homogeneous conditions the mean speed follows the fundamental diagram alpha^3 beta^3 (1 - rho)^2 / (alpha^2 beta^2 (1 - rho)^2 + (1 - c alpha beta) rho^2) (Eq. 6.30), which a single cell spanning the street reproduces. The default is the counterflow of section 6.5.2, 50 walkers uniformly distributed in a street periodic along its length, for which the book reports segregation into lanes, measured by the band index of Eq. 6.31, most pronounced at beta = 0.5. The densities are counted on cells of side cell, an assumption of this particle method. The venue is a distinct system from EvacuationSystem, a network of rooms. One step is a tenth of a time unit.

    """Return the transition table of encounters between citizens, `A[h, k, i]`.

    A citizen of class `h` who meets a more criminal citizen moves one class up with probability `attraction`, and one
    who meets a more honest citizen moves one class down with probability `redemption`.

        redemption:
            The probability that honesty redeems, a number or a parameter.

        table:
            The transition probabilities, which sum to one over the outcomes.

        >>> contagion_table(3, 0.3, 0.1)[0, 2].astype(float).tolist()
        [0.7, 0.3, 0.0]
    """
    table = np.zeros((classes, classes, classes), dtype=object)
    for candidate in range(classes):
        for field in range(classes):
            if field > candidate:
                table[candidate, field, candidate + 1] += attraction
            elif field < candidate:
                table[candidate, field, candidate - 1] += redemption
            table[candidate, field, candidate] += 1 - table[candidate, field].sum()
    return table

    def crowding_table(rooms: int, speeds: int, step: int) -> tuple[npt.NDArray[np.float64], ...]:
    """Return the rate pattern and the transition table of crowding encounters.

    The encounters change the speed class by one step. With `step = -1`, two pedestrians in the same room meet and the
    candidate slows down one class, so crowded rooms walk slower. With `step = 1`, a pedestrian meets the exit signs and
    speeds up one class.

    Args:
        rooms:
            The number of rooms.

        speeds:
            The number of speed classes.

        step:
            `-1` to slow down in encounters within a room, `1` to speed up in encounters with the exit signs.

    Returns:
        pattern:
            The rate pattern, 1 for the encounters that act.

        table:
            The transition table.

    Examples:
        >>> from skcomplex.systems.social.crowds import crowding_table
        >>> crowding_table(1, 2, 1)[0].ravel().tolist()
        [1.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    """
    sites = (rooms + 1) * speeds
    fields = sites if step < 0 else 1
    pattern = np.zeros((sites, fields))
    table = np.zeros((sites, fields, sites))
    for site in range(sites):
        room, speed = divmod(site, speeds)
        outcome = min(max(speed + step, 0), speeds - 1)
        table[site, :, room * speeds + outcome] = 1.0
        if room == rooms or outcome == speed:
            continue
        if step > 0:
            pattern[site, 0] = 1.0
        else:
            pattern[site, room * speeds : (room + 1) * speeds] = 1.0
    return pattern, table

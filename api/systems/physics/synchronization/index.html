    AdaptiveKuramotoSystem(n_oscillators=50, *, alpha=0.3 * math.pi, beta=0.23 * math.pi, epsilon=0.01, omega=0.0, seed=0, label='system')

    Bases: BaseSystem

    Globally coupled phase oscillators with adaptive coupling weights.

    The weights adapt to the phase differences.

    The oscillators follow the Kuramoto-Sakaguchi model theta_i' = omega - (1 / N) Σ_j kappa_ij sin(theta_i - theta_j + alpha), and every weight of the directed link from j to i adapts as kappa_ij' = -epsilon (kappa_ij + sin(theta_i - theta_j + beta)), Eqs. 28-29 of Berner et al. The weights are states of a group on the edges of the complete directed graph. Even identical oscillators split into frequency clusters: groups whose members share one mean frequency, strongly coupled within and weakly coupled across, with the weights inside a cluster near -sin(theta_i - theta_j + beta). The phases and the weights start uniformly at random in [0, 2 pi) and [-1, 1]. The system has the phases nodes.theta and the weights links.kappa, one per directed link.

    Bases: BaseSystem

    Two chaotic maps coupled by aggregate averaging.

    The maps synchronize their chaos for strong coupling.

    Each map iterates on the aggregate of its own value and the other's, x_i(t + 1) = f(a_i) with a_1 = (1 - kappa) x_1(t) + kappa x_2(t - T) and a_2 = (1 - kappa) x_2(t) + kappa x_1(t - T), for the delay T. The logistic map is f(a) = r a (1 - a). Without delay, its synchronized orbit is stable for kappa > (r - 1) / (2 r), which is 3 / 8 for r = 4, and the maps agree in one step for kappa = 1 / 2. The Bernoulli shift f(a) = r a mod 1 has the constant slope r, so a difference d of the maps follows d(t + 1) = r ((1 - kappa) d(t) - kappa d(t - T)) exactly, and the maps synchronize when every root of z^(T + 1) - r (1 - kappa) z^T + r kappa lies in the unit disk, which for T = 0 is |r (1 - 2 kappa)| < 1. The delay shrinks the window of synchronizing couplings: for r = 1.5 it is 0.17 < kappa < 0.83 without delay, 0.17 < kappa < 0.66 for T = 1, and 0.17 < kappa < 0.44 for T = 2, and it closes for T >= 5. Before the start, the delayed values equal the initial values. An integer slope such as 2 loses one binary digit of the value per step in floating point and falls onto 0, so a slope that is not an integer, such as 1.5, keeps the chaos. The system has the values first.x and second.x, and their lags first.x_lag<j> and second.x_lag<j> for a delay.

    Phases on the nodes and links of a clique complex.

    The phases are coupled through its incidence by order parameters.

    Node phases follow theta' = omega + K R_links codifferential(sin(d theta)). Link phases follow phi' = omega + K R_nodes d(sin(codifferential(phi))) - K codifferential(sin(d phi)). With the signs of codifferential, these are the Nodes-Links system of Ghorbanchian et al. (2021), Eq. 6 of the review of Majhi, Perc, and Ghosh (2022, arXiv 2203.06601 §III), which is the source at hand. Here R_nodes is the order parameter of the node phases and R_links that of the projection of the link phases on the nodes. The review takes frequencies of mean Omega and standard deviation 1 / tau, with Omega = 2 and tau = 1 for its Fig. 2, on complexes of 500 nodes. The mean 0, the size, and the random graph are choices of this system.

    The Nodes-Links-Triangles system, model='NLT', Eq. 8 of the review, scales the coupling of the link phases through the nodes by R_nodes R_up and through the triangles by R_links, where R_up is the order parameter of the projection of the link phases on the triangles. Every projection then couples to another, so all three order parameters jump together at one explosive transition, while in the Nodes-Links system R_up moves on its own. The system has a group triangles of unit weights on the faces for the Nodes-Links-Triangles system, which only gives the order parameter of the projection on the triangles.

    WinfreeSystem(n_oscillators=50, *, K=0.5, spread=0.1, seed=0, label='system')

    Bases: BaseSystem

    The Winfree system.

    The system follows theta_i' = omega_i + (K / N) sum_j P(theta_j) R(theta_i), with the sum over all j.

    The influence P(theta) = 1 + cos(theta) and the sensitivity R(theta) = -sin(theta) make every oscillator respond to the mean influence of the population, which every oscillator reads as a group mean. The natural frequencies are spread evenly over [1 - spread, 1 + spread]. Weak coupling leaves the oscillators incoherent, moderate coupling locks them to one frequency, and strong coupling stops them, which is oscillator death. The system has the phases oscillators.theta and the order parameter order.

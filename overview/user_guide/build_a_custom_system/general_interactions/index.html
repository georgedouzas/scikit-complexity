    General interactions

    Most processes you want to describe already exist as a general interaction. You pick the class that does the mathematical operation you need, bind it to your elements, and pass the functions that make it your process.

    from skcomplex.core.components.elements import CompartmentElement
from skcomplex.core.components.interactions import RateInteraction
from skcomplex.core.components.system import System
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import Simulation

decay = RateInteraction('decay', target='stock', state='n', rate=lambda s: -0.5 * s.target.n)
system = System(elements=[CompartmentElement('stock', n=8.0)], interactions=[decay])
round(float(Simulation(system, dt=0.01).prepare().run(until=2.0).trajectory_.values('stock.n')[-1]), 3)

    2.943

    skcomplex.core.components.interactions holds one general class per mathematical operation: a rate, a gradient, a map, a coupling, a jump, a transition, a choice, a movement, a transfer, a grouping. Find the operation you need in the list, then follow the link to its subsection.

    • Change a state continuously by a rate, towards a goal, or with noise: RateInteraction and RelaxationInteraction, in Rates and relaxation.
    • Move states down a potential, or write the mechanics of a Lagrangian or a Hamiltonian: GradientInteraction, EulerLagrangeInteraction, and HamiltonianInteraction, in Gradients and mechanics.
    • Move a particle along the geodesics of a curved metric, or evolve a metric ansatz by the Einstein equations: PseudoRiemannianSpace, GeodesicInteraction, and EinsteinInteraction, in Curved spacetimes.
    • Conserve, carry, or spread a quantity over a field: ConservationInteraction, AdvectionInteraction, and DiffusionInteraction, in Conservation laws.
    • Update a state in steps, search by trial and error, or run a cellular automaton or a cascade: MapInteraction, SignAdjustmentInteraction, LocalRuleInteraction, and CascadeInteraction, in Discrete time.
    • Couple members over the edges of a lattice or a network, over a group, or over close pairs: CouplingInteraction and PairCouplingInteraction, in Coupling over a structure.
    • Move particles through a viscous fluid by the forces, torques, and active slip of all of them: MobilityInteraction, in Coupling over a structure.
    • Fire random events or chemical reactions: JumpInteraction and ReactionInteraction, in Jumps and reactions.
    • Move a member between discrete values, such as susceptible and infected: TransitionInteraction, in Transitions between values.
    • Give the encounters of a kinetic system: EncounterInteraction, in Encounters of a kinetic system.
    • Reset states when a condition crosses zero, or at chosen times: CrossingInteraction and ImpulseInteraction, in Crossings and impulses.
    • Sample configurations by Monte Carlo: SamplingInteraction, in Monte Carlo sampling and Row scores and zero temperature.
    • Set a state from the time or the random stream of the step: DriveLaw, in Drives that read the step.
    • Run an operation as symbolic laws or as agent rules: the form and schedule arguments, in Agent and symbolic forms.
    • Choose, imitate, learn, or evolve: ChoiceInteraction, ImitationInteraction, ReinforcementInteraction, and EvolutionInteraction, in Choice and learning.
    • Move agents, transfer amounts, create and remove agents, form groups, match partners, or rewire a network: MovementInteraction, TransferInteraction, LifeCycleInteraction, GroupingInteraction, MatchingInteraction, FeedbackInteraction, and TopologyInteraction, in Agents, groups, and exchange.
    • Place agents in three dimensions or in regions such as census tracts: ContinuousSpace and MovementInteraction, in Agents in three dimensions and on region layers.
    • Write laws with Hill functions, delays, pulses, or group reductions: skcomplex.core.components.laws.utils, in Law utilities.
    • Evolve a tensor state of a field: StateSpec(tensor=...), in Tensor fields.
    • Do something no general class covers: the binding bases, in When no general class fits.

    How the general classes are configured

    A class names what it does, never what a state means. No core class or argument names a force, a position, a price, an infection, or a harvest. You configure a class by the roles it binds, the names of the states it reads and writes, and the functions you pass.

    • target is the role whose states the laws change. sources are roles the laws only read.
    • state, states, read, and write name the states, so one class serves any meaning. A force is a rate on a state of order 2, and a mass is its divisor.
    • Functions are callables of the namespace s that return symbolic expressions, such as rate=lambda s: -s.k * s.target.n. Where an argument allows it, a number stands for a constant function.
    • divisor divides what a rate, a gradient, or a pair coupling adds to the highest derivative, such as a mass, a friction, or a capacitance. It is a named argument, not part of the function, so a backend such as LAMMPS can read it.
    • A variant is a string checked against the constants of its module, such as mode='increment'. A variant that carries an algorithm of its own is a frozen spec instead, such as the rule of a matching.

    What enters the core

    A class enters the core when it is a fundamental paradigm, such as a variational principle, a conservation law, a Markov jump process, a reaction network, or imitation dynamics, and when it applies in principle to other domains than the one that first needs it. How many fields use it today does not decide it.

    The case of one field is a thin subclass of a general class in the catalog, even when it only renames arguments. It lives with the one system that uses it, or with the code its field shares when several systems use it. These subclasses double as examples of how to build a part of your own, and the page of each system shows them. The interactions of lotka_volterra, for example, are all rate interactions:

    from skcomplex.core.components.interactions import RateInteraction
from skcomplex.systems import create_system

system = create_system('lotka_volterra')
{type(part).__name__: isinstance(part, RateInteraction) for part in system.interactions}

    {'ExponentialGrowthInteraction': True, 'PredationInteraction': False, 'MortalityInteraction': True}

    Elements of an interaction

    The general interactions act on a few general elements, so you rarely need an element class of your own.

    from skcomplex.core.components.elements import CompartmentElement, PointElement
from skcomplex.core.components.spaces import ContinuousSpace
from skcomplex.core.components.system import System
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import Simulation

system = System(
    elements=[CompartmentElement('stock', n=5.0), PointElement('point', x_d1=[1.0, 0.0])],
    space=ContinuousSpace(n_dim=2),
)
trajectory = Simulation(system, dt=1.0).prepare().run(until=2.0).trajectory_
trajectory.values('stock.n')[-1], trajectory.values('point.x')[-1].tolist()

    (5.0, [2.0, 0.0])
    • Element declares states and parameters. GroupElement holds many identical members as arrays. FieldElement and LatticeElement place one member at every site of a space.
    • CompartmentElement is a stock with one state n, such as a species, a compartment, or a queue. Since states are declared per class, a system renames n by a thin subclass.
    • DensityElement is the density f of one activity class of a kinetic system.
    • PointElement is a member with a vector state x of order 2 in a continuous space. Its first derivative is x_d1, so a backend such as LAMMPS reads the velocity of a point from its declared states.

    Continuous time

    These classes write differential equations, with or without noise.

    Rates and relaxation

    Use RateInteraction to add any rate to a state, as the decay at the top of this section does, and RelaxationInteraction to pull a state towards a goal.

    RateInteraction(target, state, rate, sources=(), noise=None, divisor=None, hurst=None) adds rate(s), and noise(s) dW, to the highest derivative of a state of any order. A Hurst index hurst makes the noise fractional.

    from skcomplex.core.components.elements import CompartmentElement
from skcomplex.core.components.interactions import RelaxationInteraction
from skcomplex.core.components.system import System
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import Simulation

system = System(elements=[CompartmentElement('belief', n=0.0)], interactions=[smoothing])
round(float(Simulation(system, dt=0.01).prepare().run(until=5.0).trajectory_.values('belief.n')[-1]), 3)

    10.0

    RelaxationInteraction(target, state, goal, time, noise=None) relaxes a state towards a goal at the rate (goal - x) / time. With noise, it is an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process.

    Gradients and mechanics

    Use GradientInteraction when a state moves down a potential. On a state of order 1 it is a gradient flow:

    from skcomplex.core.components.elements import CompartmentElement, PointElement

from skcomplex.core.components.spaces import ContinuousSpace
from skcomplex.core.components.system import System
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import Simulation

well = GradientInteraction('well', target='walker', states='n', potential=lambda s: s.target.n**2 / 2)

round(float(Simulation(flow, dt=0.01).prepare().run(until=1.0).trajectory_.values('walker.n')[-1]), 4)

    0.3679

    On a state of order 2, such as the position of a point, the same class gives Newton's equations:

    spring = GradientInteraction('spring', target='point', states='x', potential=lambda s: s.target.x * s.target.x / 2)
newton = System(elements=[PointElement('point', x=[1.0])], interactions=[spring], space=ContinuousSpace(n_dim=1))
round(float(Simulation(newton, dt=0.01).prepare().run(until=3.1416).trajectory_.values('point.x')[-1][0]), 3)

    -1.0

    GradientInteraction(target, states, potential, sources=(), divisor=None, variational=False, conserved=False) also gives the variational or conserved dynamics of a field. The divisor is a name, a function of the member, or a mapping of one divisor per state.

    Curved spacetimes

    Use PseudoRiemannianSpace and GeodesicInteraction when a particle falls freely in a metric you know, such as a planet around a static mass in the Schwarzschild metric:

    from sage.all__sagemath_symbolics import SR, diagonal_matrix, pi, sin

from skcomplex.core.components.elements import PointElement
from skcomplex.core.components.interactions import GeodesicInteraction
from skcomplex.core.components.spaces import PseudoRiemannianSpace
from skcomplex.core.components.system import System
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import Simulation
from skcomplex.core.engine.solvers import ScipyODESolver

t, r, theta, phi = SR.var('t r theta phi')
lapse = 1 - 2 / r
metric = diagonal_matrix(SR, [-lapse, 1 / lapse, r**2, r**2 * sin(theta) ** 2])
spacetime = PseudoRiemannianSpace(metric, (t, r, theta, phi), curvature=False)
dilation = 1 / (1 - 3 / 20) ** 0.5
planet = PointElement('planet', x=[0.0, 20.0, pi / 2, 0.0], x_d1=[dilation, 0.0, 0.0, dilation / 20**1.5])
fall = GeodesicInteraction('fall', target='planet', space=spacetime)
system = System(elements=[planet], interactions=[fall], space=spacetime)
solver = ScipyODESolver(method='DOP853', rtol=1e-10, atol=1e-10)
trajectory = Simulation(system, dt=10.0, solver=solver).prepare().run(until=500.0).trajectory_
round(float(trajectory.values('planet.x')[-1][1]), 6)

    20.0

    PseudoRiemannianSpace(metric, coordinates, curvature=True) takes a symmetric Sage matrix and its coordinates, and prepare derives through EinsteinPy the Christoffel symbols christoffel_, and with curvature the tensors riemann_, ricci_, ricci_scalar_, and einstein_. It derives them again only when the metric, the coordinates, or curvature change. The space is an unbounded continuous space with one dimension per coordinate, so a point on it has one component per coordinate. GeodesicInteraction(target, state='x', space) gives that vector state of order 2 the geodesic equation x''^mu = -Γ^mu_(alpha beta) x'^alpha x'^beta, with the system time as the affine parameter. The initial four-velocity decides the kind of geodesic: a norm g_(mu nu) x'^mu x'^nu of -1 is a massive particle on its proper time, and 0 is a light ray. The circular orbit above, of radius 20 M, keeps its radius.

    Use EinsteinInteraction when the metric itself is the unknown, reduced by a symmetry to functions of time, such as the scale factor a(t) of a flat universe of dust:

    import math

from sage.all__sagemath_symbolics import SR, diagonal_matrix, function, sin

from skcomplex.core.components.elements import Element

from skcomplex.core.components.system import System
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import Simulation

class UniverseElement(Element):
    def __init__(
        self,
        label,
        *,
        a: Annotated[float, StateSpec(order=2)] = 1.0,
        a_d1: float = 1.0,
        rho: Annotated[float, ParameterSpec()] = 3 / (8 * math.pi),
    ):
        super().__init__(label)
        self.a = a
        self.a_d1 = a_d1
        self.rho = rho


t, r, theta, phi = SR.var('t r theta phi')
a = function('a')(t)
metric = diagonal_matrix(SR, [-1, a**2, a**2 * r**2, a**2 * r**2 * sin(theta) ** 2])
ansatz = PseudoRiemannianSpace(metric, (t, r, theta, phi))


def dust(s):
    return diagonal_matrix(SR, [-s.target.rho / s.target.a**3, 0, 0, 0])


expansion = EinsteinInteraction(
    'expansion', target='universe', states='a', space=ansatz, components=[(1, 1)], source=dust
)
system = System(elements=[UniverseElement('universe')], interactions=[expansion])
trajectory = Simulation(system, dt=0.1).prepare().run(until=2.0).trajectory_
round(float(trajectory.values('universe.a')[-1]), 4)

    EinsteinInteraction(target, states, space, components, source=None, coupling=8π, cosmological_constant=0.0, time=0, axes=None) names every function of the metric after a state or parameter of the target. It takes one component (mu, nu) of the mixed equations G^mu_nu + Λ δ^mu_nu = κ T^mu_nu per state, and solves them for the second time derivatives. The source returns the mixed energy-momentum tensor, here dust of density ρ / a³, so the scale factor grows as (3 t / 2 + 1)^(2/3). The other components, such as the Friedmann equation G^t_t, are constraints the initial values satisfy. A function of a spatial coordinate too, such as the radius R(t, r) of the shells of a dust ball, needs that coordinate on a lattice axis of a field, axes={1: 0}, and its derivatives become partial of the field. An equation that mixes time and space derivatives, or keeps a coordinate, raises an error.

    Conservation laws

    Use these classes for a quantity of a field that is only moved around, never created or lost, except by a source. The example runs Burgers' flux, a drift, and diffusion on a periodic finite-volume field, and the total stays the same in each case.

    from typing import Annotated

import numpy as np

from skcomplex.core.components.elements import Element, FieldElement
from skcomplex.core.components.interactions import AdvectionInteraction, ConservationInteraction, DiffusionInteraction
from skcomplex.core.components.spaces import LatticeSpace
from skcomplex.core.components.system import System
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import Simulation
from skcomplex.core.specs import StateSpec


class CellElement(Element):
    def __init__(self, label, *, u: Annotated[float, StateSpec()] = 0.0):
        super().__init__(label)
        self.u = u


space = LatticeSpace((50,), spacing=0.1, boundary='periodic')
values = {'u': 1.0 + np.sin(2 * np.pi * np.arange(50) / 50)}
interactions = {
    'burgers': ConservationInteraction('burgers', target='wave', state='u', flux=lambda s: s.target.u**2 / 2),
    'drift': AdvectionInteraction('drift', target='wave', state='u', drift=lambda s: 1.0),
    'heat': DiffusionInteraction('heat', target='wave', state='u', coefficient=0.1),
}
totals = {}
for label, interaction in interactions.items():
    field = FieldElement('wave', element=CellElement, space=space, values=values, discretization='finite-volume')
    system = System(elements=[field], interactions=[interaction])
    total = Simulation(system, dt=0.01).prepare().run(until=0.5).trajectory_.values('wave.u')[-1].sum()
    totals[label] = round(float(total), 6)
totals

The flux laws of one field form one system. With riemann=None, it lowers to the divergence of the flux on spectral and finite-difference fields.
    • AdvectionInteraction(target, state, drift) carries a state by a drift a, which is the flux a u.
    • DiffusionInteraction(target, state, coefficient) is the flux minus a coefficient times the gradient.

    heights = Simulation(system, dt=1.0).prepare().run(until=10.0).trajectory_.values('spheres.x')[..., 2]
    return float(heights[0, 0] - heights[-1, 0]) / 10 * 6 * np.pi

    (1.0, 1.269)

    MobilityInteraction(target, position='x', orientation=None, tensor='rotne-prager-yamakawa', form='rate', force, torque, velocity, stresslet, dipole, potential, cutoff, root='cholesky', radius, viscosity, temperature, dt) moves the spheres of a group by the grand mobility M, the linear map from the forces and torques on all spheres to their velocities and angular velocities. A lone sphere moves at F / (6 pi eta a), and the second sphere above adds the fraction 3 a / (4 r) + a^3 / (2 r^3) to the speed of the first, about a quarter at the distance r = 3.

    • The tensor is 'oseen', the flow of a point force, 'rotne-prager-yamakawa', which adds the finite size of the spheres and stays positive definite when they overlap, or 'free-draining', which couples no pair. The rotlet couples torques to velocities and forces to angular velocities, and an orientation state turns at Omega x p.
    • The force, the torque, an intrinsic velocity, a stresslet, and a potential dipole are functions of the namespace of a member. The stresslet and the dipole are force-free sources whose flows move and turn the other spheres, so together with the intrinsic velocity they carry the active slip of a microswimmer. A pair potential(s, r) of the distance adds its forces within cutoff.
    • In form='map', in discrete time, every step is the Ermak-McCammon step x + M F dt + sqrt(2 kT dt) B xi of Brownian dynamics, where B B^T = M. root='cholesky' factors M, and root='lanczos' applies a Krylov approximation of its square root with the matrix-free product, for larger groups. The time of the namespace then counts steps.
    • Positions in the plane are the plane z = 0 of a three-dimensional fluid. The fluid is unbounded, every evaluation costs O(N^2), and Cholesky costs O(N^3).

    The library has squirmers, spherical microswimmers whose slip modes make them pushers or pullers, and hydrodynamically_coupled_traps, two Brownian spheres in blinking optical tweezers whose free steps correlate through the mobility.

    ContinuousSpace(regions=...) holds layers of regions in a space of two sides. It is a mapping from the name of a layer to a sequence of Shapely polygons in the coordinates of the space. prepare keeps one STRtree per layer in regions_. For a region that is not a valid polygon, it raises SpaceError, which names the layer and the region.

    • SystemView.region(layer, index) returns the polygon of a region, and SystemView.regions(layer) the number of regions of a layer.

    • AgentsVisual draws every layer once as static LineStrips2D under the agents.

    Tensor fields

    Use a tensor state when a field carries a matrix at every site, such as the orientation of a liquid crystal or a stress. The example stirs a symmetric traceless tensor by the flow it drives, and diffusion and decay shrink it:

    from typing import Annotated

import numpy as np

from skcomplex.core.components.elements import Element, FieldElement
from skcomplex.core.components.laws import RateLaw
from skcomplex.core.components.laws.utils import (
    advection,
    inverse_laplacian,
    laplacian,
    leray_projection,
    tensor_divergence,
)
from skcomplex.core.components.spaces import LatticeSpace
from skcomplex.core.components.system import System
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import Simulation
from skcomplex.core.specs import StateSpec


class StirredElement(Element):
    def __init__(self, label, Q: Annotated[object, StateSpec(tensor='traceless')] = 0.0):
        super().__init__(label)
        self.Q = Q

    def _get_laws(self, s):
        force = tensor_divergence(s.Q)
        velocity = leray_projection(force.apply_map(lambda item: inverse_laplacian(-item)))
        return [RateLaw(s.Q, -s.Q.apply_map(lambda item: advection(item, velocity)) + s.Q.apply_map(laplacian) - s.Q)]


size = 16
spacing = 2 * np.pi / size
x, y = np.meshgrid(np.arange(size) * spacing, np.arange(size) * spacing, indexing='ij')
start = np.stack([0.1 * np.cos(x).ravel(), 0.1 * np.sin(y).ravel()], axis=1)
film = FieldElement(
    'film',
    element=StirredElement,
    space=LatticeSpace((size, size), spacing=spacing),
    values={'Q': start},
    discretization='spectral',
)
trajectory = Simulation(System(elements=[film]), dt=0.01).prepare().run(until=0.2).trajectory_
bool(np.max(np.abs(trajectory.values('film.Q')[-1])) < np.max(np.abs(start)))

    StateSpec(tensor=symmetry) declares a rank-2 tensor state of a field on a lattice of 2 or 3 dimensions. The symmetry is 'full', 'symmetric', 'antisymmetric', or 'traceless', which means symmetric and traceless.

    • The element stores only the independent components, such as Q_xx and Q_xy for a symmetric traceless state in 2 dimensions. Initial values give one value per independent component.
    • Laws read s.Q as a Sage matrix whose dependent entries are written in terms of the independent ones. A RateLaw on it takes a matrix.

    The operations on vectors and tensors are:

    • vector_gradient(v) is the matrix partial(v_i, j), and tensor_divergence(T) the vector of the sums of partial(T_ij, j) over j.
    • symmetric_part(M) and antisymmetric_part(M) split a matrix, so the strain rate of a velocity is symmetric_part(vector_gradient(u)).
    • T.apply_map(laplacian) is the Laplacian of a matrix, entry by entry, as v.apply_map(laplacian) is that of a vector.
    • The transport of a tensor or a vector by a flow advects it, rotates it with the vorticity, and aligns it with the strain rate. It is an expression of advection, vector_gradient, symmetric_part, and antisymmetric_part, and a law subtracts it from its rate.
    • leray_projection(v) is the divergence-free part of a vector, v - gradient(inverse_laplacian(divergence(v))). It is exact on spectral fields of periodic lattices. A velocity odd about the middle of an axis, set by the symmetry of the field, gives walls there.

    The boundary of a field takes a matrix of the symmetry of the state as the fixed value ('fixed', matrix), which gives every edge of the lattice that matrix. PassCompiler reports a rate law that breaks the declared symmetry with the code tensor-symmetry, and a tensor operation given the wrong shape with the code law-domain.

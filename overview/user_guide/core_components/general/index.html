    General realizations

    skcomplex.systems.general holds the recipes that systems of several fields share, such as a nonnegative amount, a reaction, an agent that moves, or a market that matches buyers with sellers. A recipe is a part written with the public parts of the core alone, so each one is also an example of how to build a part of your own. The core never imports these recipes, and every field imports them freely. How the parts fit shows the parts of the core that the recipes are written with, and the section Where a part goes at the end of this page gives the rule that decides whether a new part is a recipe.

    The package has one subpackage per concept, and every recipe is imported from its subpackage.

    Subpackage Concept Recipes
    elements amounts and bodies NonnegativeElement, PointElement
    flows chaotic flows LorenzElement
    reactions reactions at a product propensity ReactionInteraction, ReactionNetworkSystem
    kinetics encounters of distributions EncounterInteraction
    dynamics systems given as equations DynamicalSystem, NetworkDynamicalSystem, ReactionDiffusionSystem, StockFlowSystem
    agents agent rules MovementInteraction, ChoiceInteraction, ImitationInteraction, EvolutionInteraction, BirthDeathInteraction
    matching markets that pair two groups MatchingInteraction, its rule specs, and MatchSpec

    NonnegativeElement

    A NonnegativeElement holds one number x that stays at zero or above, such as a count of people or the moles of a chemical species. It is an Element whose one state carries the domain [0, +oo), and it returns no laws of its own. Here 100 infected people recover at a rate of 0.1 a day through a RateInteraction.

    from skcomplex.core.components.interactions import RateInteraction
from skcomplex.core.components.systems import CompositeSystem
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import simulate
from skcomplex.systems.general.elements import NonnegativeElement

infected = NonnegativeElement('infected', x=100.0)
recovery = RateInteraction('recovery', target='infected', state='x', rate=lambda s: -0.1 * s.target.x)
epidemic = CompositeSystem(elements=[infected], interactions=[recovery])
simulate(epidemic, until=10, dt=5.0).get_values('infected.x').round(1).tolist()

    [100.0, 60.7, 36.8]

    The infected fall from 100 to 60.7 after five days and to 36.8 after ten.

    The same element holds the moles of a chemical species in a reactor. A species that decays at the rate 0.5 loses about two thirds of its amount in two seconds.

    species = NonnegativeElement('A', x=2.0)
decay = RateInteraction('decay', target='A', state='x', rate=lambda s: -0.5 * s.target.x)
simulate(CompositeSystem(elements=[species], interactions=[decay]), until=2, dt=1.0).get_values('A.x').round(3).tolist()

    [2.0, 1.213, 0.736]

    The amount falls from 2.0 to 1.213 and then to 0.736, which is 2 exp(-1).

    How the engine runs it. The element adds no laws. The compiler reads its state as it reads any annotated state, and the laws of the interactions that target it form the equations a solver integrates.

    How it combines. It is the species of ReactionInteraction and ReactionNetworkSystem, and a GroupElement of it on the sites of a lattice is the distribution that EncounterInteraction reads.

    Limits and errors. It holds one number. A system that tracks several numbers per member declares its own Element. The check of the domain runs when the system compiles, so a negative initial value raises a ValidationError from simulate.

    from skcomplex.core.errors import ValidationError

try:
    simulate(CompositeSystem(elements=[NonnegativeElement('A', x=-1.0)]), until=1, dt=1.0)
except ValidationError as error:
    message = str(error)
message

    The system is not valid, with 1 error(s):
- A: A.x = -1.0 is not in its domain [0, +oo). Correct the component as the message describes. [value]

    The message names the value A.x = -1.0 and the domain [0, +oo) that does not hold it. The fix is an initial value of zero or above.

    A field realization. Biology names it a compartment of an epidemic and declares the same state again, so the docstring of the subclass can say that x counts people.

    PointElement

    A PointElement is a body that moves in a continuous space by its acceleration. Its state x is a vector of order 2, the position, and the companion state x_d1 holds the velocity. Here a ball leaves the origin at the velocity (1, 2) and falls under a gravity of 1, which a RateInteraction gives as the second derivative of x.

    from skcomplex.core.components.interactions import RateInteraction
from skcomplex.core.components.spaces import ContinuousSpace
from skcomplex.core.components.systems import CompositeSystem
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import simulate
from skcomplex.systems.general.elements import PointElement

ball = PointElement('ball', x=[0.0, 0.0], x_d1=[1.0, 2.0])
gravity = RateInteraction('gravity', target='ball', state='x', rate=lambda s: [0.0, -1.0])
flight = CompositeSystem(elements=[ball], interactions=[gravity], space=ContinuousSpace(n_dim=2))
simulate(flight, until=2.0, dt=1.0).get_values('ball.x').round(3).tolist()

    [[0.0, 0.0], [1.0, 1.5], [2.0, 2.0]]

    The ball reaches (1, 1.5) after one second and (2, 2) after two, the parabola (t, 2t - t^2 / 2).

    A star that drifts through a cluster at a constant velocity is the same element with no force on it.

    star = PointElement('star', x_d1=[1.0, 0.0])
drift = CompositeSystem(elements=[star], space=ContinuousSpace(n_dim=2))
simulate(drift, until=2.0, dt=1.0).get_values('star.x')[-1].tolist()

    [2.0, 0.0]

    The star stands at (2, 0) after two units of time.

    How the engine runs it. The laws on x give its second derivative, and the compiler adds the first-order equations of the pair x and x_d1. A backend for molecular dynamics, such as LAMMPS, reads the velocity of a point from the declared state x_d1, and the boundary of the space acts on x.

    How it combines. It lives in a ContinuousSpace. A PairInteraction adds forces between points, such as those of a gas or of a flock.

    Limits and errors. Its state is a vector, so the system needs a space with a dimension, and simulate raises a SpaceError without one.

    from skcomplex.core.errors import SpaceError

except SpaceError as error:
    message = str(error)
message

    The system is not valid, with 1 error(s):
- p: p.x is a vector or a tensor, so the system that holds it or one of its parents must have a space. Give the system a space whose kind the component supports. [space-missing]

    The message says that p.x is a vector, so the system that holds it must have a space. The fix is space=ContinuousSpace(n_dim=2).

    A field realization. Physics names the point an atom and fixes its start at rest.

        super().__init__(label, x=x, x_d1=0.0, visual=visual)

    LorenzElement

    A LorenzElement follows the Lorenz flow x' = sigma (y - x), y' = x (rho - z) - y, z' = x y - beta z, which Lorenz derived from convection in the atmosphere. It is an Element that returns three RateLaw laws. For sigma = 10, rho = 28, and beta = 8/3 its runs never settle and never repeat.

    from skcomplex.core.components.systems import CompositeSystem
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import simulate
from skcomplex.systems.general.flows import LorenzElement

trajectory = simulate(CompositeSystem(elements=[LorenzElement('lorenz')]), until=1.0, dt=0.5)
trajectory.get_values('lorenz.x').round(3).tolist()

    [1.0, 1.198, -9.379]

    The state x moves from 1.0 to 1.198 and swings to -9.379 after one unit of time.

    Below the critical value of rho the same flow describes a water wheel that settles into a steady rotation. With rho = 10 the state z settles at rho - 1.

    wheel = simulate(CompositeSystem(elements=[LorenzElement('wheel', rho=10.0)]), until=20.0, dt=10.0)
wheel.get_values('wheel.z').round(3).tolist()

    [1.0, 9.023, 9.0]

    The state z passes 9.023 at time 10 and rests at 9.0 at time 20.

    How the engine runs it. The three rates form an ordinary differential equation that ScipyODESolver integrates.

    How it combines. It runs alone as a model, or it drives a response system through an interaction that reads its states, as in studies of chaotic synchronization.

    Limits and errors. Its parameters are sigma, rho, and beta, and set_params raises a ValueError for any other name.

    try:
    LorenzElement('lorenz').set_params(r=1.0)
except ValueError as error:
    message = str(error)
message

    Invalid parameter 'r' for estimator LorenzElement(label='lorenz'). Valid parameters are: ['beta', 'label', 'rho', 'sigma', 'visual', 'x', 'y', 'z'].

    The message lists the valid parameters beta, label, rho, sigma, visual, x, y, and z. The fix is rho=....

    A field realization. LorenzSystem of mathematics wraps the element in a system class that takes the parameters of the model, and the chaotic synchronization of physics drives a response system with it.

    from skcomplex.systems.mathematics.dynamical_systems import LorenzSystem

LorenzSystem(rho=20.0).prepare().system_.elements[0].rho

    20.0

    The system builds one LorenzElement with rho = 20.0.

    ReactionInteraction

    A ReactionInteraction moves units from its reactants to its products, at a propensity that is the rate constant k times the product of the reactant amounts, each raised to its coefficient. It is a JumpInteraction whose changes are the stoichiometric vector, the number of units each reaction takes from or gives to every species. A species is a pair of the path of an element and a state name. Here an epidemic runs as two reactions, infection S + I -> 2 I and recovery I -> R.

    from skcomplex.core.components.interactions import JumpInteraction
from skcomplex.core.components.systems import CompositeSystem
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import simulate
from skcomplex.systems.general.elements import NonnegativeElement
from skcomplex.systems.general.reactions import ReactionInteraction

infection = ReactionInteraction('infection', reactants={('S', 'x'): 1, ('I', 'x'): 1}, products={('I', 'x'): 2}, k=5e-4)
recovery = ReactionInteraction('recovery', reactants={('I', 'x'): 1}, products={('R', 'x'): 1}, k=0.1)
people = [NonnegativeElement('S', x=990), NonnegativeElement('I', x=10), NonnegativeElement('R')]
sir = CompositeSystem(elements=people, interactions=[infection, recovery])
trajectory = simulate(sir, until=50, dt=10.0)
infected = trajectory.get_values('I.x').round(1).tolist()
infected, float(trajectory.states[-1].sum()), issubclass(ReactionInteraction, JumpInteraction)

    ([10.0, 294.4, 400.3, 175.0, 68.6, 26.4], 1000.0000000000003, True)

    The infected rise from 10 to 400.3 at day 20 and fall to 26.4 at day 50, while the population stays at 1000. The last value confirms that the class is a JumpInteraction.

    In chemistry the same class is the law of mass action. Two species bind into a complex, A + B -> C.

    binding = ReactionInteraction('binding', reactants={('A', 'x'): 1, ('B', 'x'): 1}, products={('C', 'x'): 1}, k=0.01)
species = [NonnegativeElement('A', x=100.0), NonnegativeElement('B', x=50.0), NonnegativeElement('C')]
reactor = CompositeSystem(elements=species, interactions=[binding])
simulate(reactor, until=10, dt=5.0).get_values('C.x').round(2).tolist()

    [0.0, 47.86, 49.83]

    The complex reaches 47.86 after five seconds and 49.83 after ten, close to the 50 units of B, the reactant that runs out.

    How the engine runs it. The argument mode chooses the laws. With 'rate' the reaction is a RateLaw per species, the rate equation. With 'jump' it is a JumpLaw that fires at the propensity and moves the units of the coefficients, which the algorithm of Gillespie runs. With 'langevin' it adds to the rate a NoiseLaw whose amplitude is the square root of the propensity. The tags follow the mode.

    jumps = binding.set_params(mode='jump')
sorted(jumps.get_tags().laws), jumps.get_tags().randomness

    (['jump'], 'stochastic')

    The laws are ['jump'] and the randomness is 'stochastic'.

    How it combines. Its species are usually NonnegativeElement parts. With target, a species may be the name of a state of the target alone, and a target that is a group or a field reacts in every member. modifiers enter the propensity without changing, as a catalyst does, and propensity replaces the product form, such as a saturating rate of enzyme kinetics.

    Limits and errors. The propensity is a product of amounts unless propensity says otherwise, and a reaction needs at least one reactant or product. prepare raises a ValidationError for an empty reaction.

    from skcomplex.core.errors import ValidationError

try:
    ReactionInteraction('nothing', k=1.0).prepare()
except ValidationError as error:
    message = str(error)
message

    Reaction 'nothing' has neither reactants nor products. Give at least one of them.

    The message reads "Reaction 'nothing' has neither reactants nor products. Give at least one of them." The fix is a reactant, such as reactants={('A', 'x'): 1} for a decay.

    A field realization. Epidemiology binds the species under roles of its own names. The subclass declares the roles with RoleSpec and returns the stoichiometry from the hooks _get_reactants and _get_products.

    from typing import Annotated

from skcomplex.core.specs import RoleSpec


class InfectionInteraction(ReactionInteraction):
    def __init__(
        self, label, *, susceptible: Annotated[str, RoleSpec()], infected: Annotated[str, RoleSpec()], k=5e-4
    ):
        super().__init__(label, k=k)
        self.susceptible = susceptible
        self.infected = infected

    def _get_reactants(self):
        return {(self.susceptible, 'x'): 1, (self.infected, 'x'): 1}

    def _get_products(self):
        return {(self.infected, 'x'): 2}

compartments = [NonnegativeElement('S', x=990), NonnegativeElement('I', x=10)]
outbreak = CompositeSystem(elements=compartments, interactions=[spread])
simulate(outbreak, until=10, dt=10.0).get_values('I.x').round(1).tolist()

    Without recovery the infected reach 599.9 after ten days.

    ReactionNetworkSystem

    A ReactionNetworkSystem writes a whole network of reactions as a table, with no element or interaction to write. It takes the initial amount of every species and maps the label of every reaction to its reactants, its products, and its rate constant. Here A and B convert into each other.

    from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import simulate
from skcomplex.systems.general.reactions import ReactionNetworkSystem

isomerization = ReactionNetworkSystem(
    species={'A': 10.0, 'B': 0.0},
    reactions={'forth': ({'A': 1}, {'B': 1}, 0.3), 'back': ({'B': 1}, {'A': 1}, 0.1)},
)
round(float(simulate(isomerization, until=50.0, dt=1.0).get_values('A.x')[-1]), 3)

    2.5

    The amount of A relaxes to 2.5, which is 0.1 / (0.3 + 0.1) of the total.

    An epidemic of susceptible, infected, and recovered people is the same table with other names.

    from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import simulate
from skcomplex.systems.general.reactions import ReactionNetworkSystem

sir = ReactionNetworkSystem(
    species={'S': 990.0, 'I': 10.0, 'R': 0.0},
    reactions={'infection': ({'S': 1, 'I': 1}, {'I': 2}, 5e-4), 'recovery': ({'I': 1}, {'R': 1}, 0.1)},
)
simulate(sir, until=50, dt=10.0).get_values('I.x').round(1).tolist()

    [10.0, 294.4, 400.3, 175.0, 68.6, 26.4]

    The infected follow the curve of the epidemic of the previous section, 10, 294.4, 400.3, 175.0, 68.6, and 26.4 every ten days.

    How the engine runs it. prepare builds a CompositeSystem with one NonnegativeElement per species, named after it with its amount in x, and one ReactionInteraction per reaction. The mode writes the reactions as rate equations, 'rate', as random events of single units, 'jump', or as chemical Langevin equations, 'langevin'.

    sir.set_params(mode='jump').get_tags().randomness

    stochastic

    The jump mode makes the system 'stochastic'.

    How it combines. It is a BaseSystem, so simulate, calibrate, and the analyses take it as they take any system. A field system subclasses it and gives its parts through hooks:

    • _get_species returns the initial amount of every species by name.
    • _get_reactions returns the reaction table.
    • _get_mode returns the mode.
    • _get_interactions returns the reactions as interactions, for a realization whose reactions bind roles of their own names, such as the susceptible and infected roles of an infection.

    Limits and errors. Every propensity is a product of amounts. A reaction with another propensity goes through _get_interactions. An unknown mode raises a ValidationError from prepare.

    from skcomplex.core.errors import ValidationError

try:
    sir.set_params(mode='fast').prepare()
except ValidationError as error:
    message = str(error)
message

    Reaction 'infection' has the mode 'fast'. Use one of rate, jump, langevin.

    The message reads "Reaction 'infection' has the mode 'fast'. Use one of rate, jump, langevin."

    A field realization. A field system takes the parameters of its model under its own names. SIRSystem of biology takes beta and gamma this way. Epidemics and enzyme kinetics are both reaction networks with a product propensity, which epidemiology calls homogeneous mixing and chemistry the law of mass action, so they share the general system and differ in their realizations.

    class OutbreakSystem(ReactionNetworkSystem):

        self.gamma = gamma

    EncounterInteraction

    An EncounterInteraction models the meetings of members of two distributions in a kinetic system. A distribution is a GroupElement of NonnegativeElement with one member per class of a trait, such as the levels of an opinion. A candidate at class h meets the field at class k at the rate rate[h, k], and a conservative encounter moves it to class i with the probability kernel[h, k, i]. Here candidates at three opinion levels meet a field that pulls every one of them to the first level.

    import numpy as np

from skcomplex.core.components.elements import GroupElement
from skcomplex.core.components.spaces import LatticeSpace
from skcomplex.core.components.systems import CompositeSystem
from skcomplex.core.engine.runtime import simulate
from skcomplex.systems.general.elements import NonnegativeElement
from skcomplex.systems.general.kinetics import EncounterInteraction

kernel = np.zeros((3, 1, 3))
kernel[:, :, 0] = 1.0
gather = EncounterInteraction(
    'gather', candidate='candidates', field='field', rate=lambda s: 1.0, kernel=lambda s: kernel
)
system = CompositeSystem(
    elements=[
        GroupElement('candidates', element=NonnegativeElement, size=3, on='sites', values={'x': [0.2, 0.3, 0.5]}),
        GroupElement('field', element=NonnegativeElement, size=1, values={'x': 1.0}),
    ],
    interactions=[gather],
    space=LatticeSpace(3, boundary='fixed'),
)
np.round(simulate(system, until=4.0, dt=0.5).get_values('candidates.x')[-1], 2).tolist()

    [0.99, 0.01, 0.01]

    After four units of time 0.99 of the candidates hold the first level.

    MatchingInteraction

    A MatchingInteraction pairs requesters with providers by a rule and settles every match. Requesters state the highest value they accept in their reserve state and the quantity they want in quantity, and providers the lowest value they accept and the quantity they offer. Every match lowers the quantities of both sides and moves the states of transfers. Here four buyers meet four sellers in a market for one good, and the function trade runs one round under a given rule.

def trade(rule, on_match=None):
    bids = {'reserve': [10.0, 8.0, 6.0, 4.0], 'money': 20.0}
    asks = {'reserve': [3.0, 5.0, 7.0, 9.0], 'goods': 1.0}
    buyers = GroupElement('buyers', element=TraderElement, size=4, values=bids)
    sellers = GroupElement('sellers', element=TraderElement, size=4, values=asks)
    transfers = (('money', -1), ('goods', 1))
    market = MatchingInteraction(
        'market', requesters='buyers', providers='sellers', rule=rule, transfers=transfers, on_match=on_match
    )
    system = CompositeSystem(elements=[buyers, sellers], interactions=[market], time=DiscreteTime())
    trajectory = simulate(system, until=1, random_state=0)
    value, volume = trajectory.get_values('market.value')[-1], trajectory.get_values('market.volume')[-1]
    return round(float(value), 2), float(volume)

class QuotingElement(Element):
    def __init__(
        self,
        label,
        *,
        value: Annotated[float, StateSpec()] = 0.0,
        bid: Annotated[float, StateSpec()] = 0.0,
        ask: Annotated[float, StateSpec()] = 0.0,
        demand: Annotated[float, StateSpec()] = 0.0,
        supply: Annotated[float, StateSpec()] = 0.0,
        money: Annotated[float, StateSpec()] = 0.0,
        goods: Annotated[float, StateSpec()] = 0.0,
    ):

        self.value = value
        self.bid = bid
        self.ask = ask
        self.demand = demand
        self.supply = supply
        self.money = money
        self.goods = goods

    def _get_laws(self, s):
        return [RuleLaw(self.quote, phase='quote')]

    def quote(self, agent, system):
        agent.bid = system.rng.uniform(0, agent.value) if agent.demand else 0.0
        agent.ask = system.rng.uniform(agent.value, 2.0) if agent.supply else 2.0


class ClerkInteraction(Interaction):
    law_classes: ClassVar[tuple[type, ...]] = (RuleLaw,)
    reports: ClassVar[tuple[str, ...]] = ('traded',)

    def __init__(self, label, *, fee: Annotated[float, ParameterSpec()] = 0.0):
        super().__init__(label)
        self.fee = fee

    def _get_laws(self, s):
        return [RuleLaw(self.act, phase='trade')]

    def act(self, system):
        traded = sum(agent.goods for agent in system.get_agents('buyers'))
        system.report('traded', traded)
        system.set_parameter('fee', 0.01 * traded)

    Where a part goes

    One ordered test, the placement rule of the package layout, decides where any new part goes. The source tests check it mechanically. It reads, word for word:

    Every new part SHALL be placed by one ordered test. A structural base SHALL go to the core. A part the engine handles by type, or an operator generated from one function, SHALL go to the core under a mathematical name. Any other part is a recipe, placed by its users: skcomplex.systems.general.<concept> for several fields, systems/<field>/common/<family> for one field, its system's module for one system. The core SHALL never import skcomplex.systems.

    A field with its own term names its recipe, and general uses neutral names. One example for each step follows.

    A structural base. A base where laws run once per triangle of a cell complex goes to the core, as HyperedgeInteraction does, which runs its laws once per hyperedge, and CompositeSystem, which holds the parts of a system. Parts of every field build on them.

    A part the engine handles by type. A law that a kernel lowers goes to the core under a mathematical name. GroupingInteraction stays in the core, since a compile pass lowers it by type to kernels over random groups, and economics and social science both form groups. BilinearLaw is in the core because the compiler names it, while EncounterInteraction, which only builds bilinear laws, is a recipe.

    A recipe of several fields. An agent rule that economics and social science both use goes to a concept subpackage of skcomplex.systems.general. ChoiceInteraction returns only laws the core already runs, and the oligopoly markets of economics and the standardization of social science use it, so it lives in skcomplex.systems.general.agents.

    A recipe that reveals a missing primitive. A recipe that needs an operation the engine cannot lower reveals a missing primitive, which enters the core by the second step, and the recipe keeps no engine code. The satisfiability once had a check of its own in the compiler, engine support inside a recipe. The check that a role accepts only Boolean states became a primitive of the core, RoleSpec(domains=...), so the recipe declares its role with it and the compiler keeps no code for the class. Any interaction declares such a role the same way. Here a rule on bits accepts only Boolean states, and a group of real states fails the check of the role before any run.

from skcomplex.core.components.elements import Element, GroupElement
from skcomplex.core.components.interactions import Interaction
from skcomplex.core.components.systems import CompositeSystem
from skcomplex.core.errors import RoleElementError
from skcomplex.core.specs import RoleSpec, StateSpec
from skcomplex.core.types import ElementKind


class BitRuleInteraction(Interaction):
    def __init__(
        self,
        label,
        *,
        target: Annotated[str, RoleSpec(elements=(ElementKind.GROUP,), states=('state',), domains=('boolean',))],
        state,
    ):
        super().__init__(label)
        self.target = target
        self.state = state


class BitElement(Element):
    def __init__(self, label, *, on: Annotated[float, StateSpec()] = 0.0):
        super().__init__(label)
        self.on = on

try:

except RoleElementError as error:
    message = str(error)

    Recipes of one field

    A recipe that only one field uses lives in that field, under the name the field gives it. Its system classes are the examples to read, and the gallery example of each system runs it.

    • TopplingInteraction drives the members of a lattice and relaxes every member at or above a threshold, as in SandpileSystem of skcomplex.systems.physics.criticality. The section Cascades writes a cascade with the core CascadeLaw.
    • PolicyRuleInteraction sets parameters once per round of its phase from what happened in the round, as in BrockHommesSystem of skcomplex.systems.economics.markets. A plain Interaction that returns RuleLaw(self.act, phase=...) does the same, as the clerk of the market above shows.
    • SpacetimeSpace and GeodesicInteraction move a particle along the geodesics of a metric, as in SchwarzschildPrecessionSystem of skcomplex.systems.physics.celestial_mechanics. They need the extra relativity, which installs EinsteinPy.
    • ZoneCouplingInteraction couples agents by zones of repulsion, alignment, and attraction, as in BoidsSystem of skcomplex.systems.biology.collective_behaviour.
    • CellAdhesionInteraction samples the regions of a cellular Potts model, as in CellularPottsSystem of skcomplex.systems.biology.physiology. SequenceSamplingInteraction samples the rows of sequences, as in RNANeutralNetworksSystem of skcomplex.systems.biology.evolution. WilsonSamplingInteraction samples the matrix links of a lattice gauge field, as in LatticeYangMillsSystem of skcomplex.systems.physics.waves.
    • OptimizationInteraction moves a population of candidates through pymoo, as in EvolutionaryOptimisersSystem of skcomplex.systems.computer_science.evolutionary_computation.
    • SatisfiabilityInteraction reveals logical constraints step by step to a PySAT solver, as in PerceptronSatisfiabilitySystem of skcomplex.systems.computer_science.neural_networks.
